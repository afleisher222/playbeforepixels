## 1. The "no-guilt test": zero shame, made measurable

**Why it ranks first.** Respect in this category is won or lost on shame. Parents push back openly against screen-time shaming and against screen-free influencers. Busy Toddler's Susie Allison is a former kindergarten and first-grade teacher with a master's in early childhood education, and she popularized "sittervising", which validates parents' real limits. That is the respected model. Supporting quotes, internal only, wording [VERIFY]: JSTOR Daily, "When we fret about excess screen time as bad parenting, what we're really talking about is bad mothering…"; Undefining Motherhood, "Screen-free influencers have contributed to widespread societal pressure for parents (mothers, in particular) to do more and be better…". COMPLIANCE-GATE line 13 already says "no shame, no fear-selling", but it has no test anyone can score.

**What to do.** Turn gate line 13 into a scored test. Do not add a new checklist.
- **Fail** any line that implies a parent caused harm or treats screen use as a moral failing.
- **Fail these words:** rewiring, damage, addiction, toxic, zombie, digital drug, before it's too late.
- Frame every product as **adding** talk and play ("5 minutes of back-and-forth that fit in the bath"), never as taking something away.
- Standing line for guides and the course: *"Screens are part of real life. This is about adding more talk, not keeping score."*
- Every activity gets a **tired-parent version**: 2 minutes, no setup.
- The customer panel (task #10, plus the simulated panel in ops/ROUTINE.md) scores each product 1–5 on "made me feel judged" and 1–5 on "preachy". Anything above 2 gets revised.

**First action:** edit COMPLIANCE-GATE line 13 and the panel.md template. **Cost:** $0. About 1 hour per product inside the existing QA pass.

## 2. Editorial and evidence policy, with a public corrections log

**Why.** For an anonymous brand, this page does the trust work a visible founder would do. Careful readers will notice that most of BRAND.md's rule-5 sources are association studies. Labelling that ourselves builds credibility.
- **Observational cohorts (associations):** Madigan 2019, Heffler 2020, Kushima 2022, Takahashi 2023, Brushe 2024.
- **Hypothesis paper:** Harlé 2019.
- **Meta-analysis:** Delgado 2018 (54 studies).
- **Guidelines or reports:** WHO 2019, AAP 2016, UNESCO GEM 2023.

This page builds on commitments already in the repo: TRUST-CHECKLIST #12 (a research page and a "Report an error" email), the corrections policy in PROTECTION-PLAN defamation rule 4, and its research-hub "date reviewed" requirement.

**What the /editorial-policy page covers**
- **Who writes:** "a parent and educator". AI-assisted drafting is disclosed in plain words (TRUST-CHECKLIST #11).
- **Who reviews:** the paid panel (move 3), with credentials buyers can verify.
- **How research is chosen:** the rule-5 list, and why.
- **An evidence label beside every research claim:** *Association study: shows a link, not a cause* · *Meta-analysis* · *Guideline or report* · *Hypothesis*.
- **"Last reviewed" and "Version"** on every research-hub page and printable.
- **What we will never claim:** the rule-1 list.
- **A dated corrections log**, for example: "Oct 2026: changed X to Y after a reader pointed out Z".
- **A "You asked, we changed" section**, fed by move 7.
- No ads or affiliate links on research pages.

**How corrections run.** Corrections arrive through a "Report an error" category on the contact form. The weekly routine drafts each fix, and the founder approves it in writing.

**Internal only.** A 2025 Speech Pathology Australia conference review reportedly rated only 26.3% of 19 studies of a well-known parent-coaching program as high quality [VERIFY]. That program's name is trademarked, so it is never named publicly (rule 6). Drop "Verywell: reviewed by over 130 board-certified physicians": its only source is grokipedia, which is weak.

**First action:** draft the page from TRUST-CHECKLIST #12. **Cost:** 4–6 hours once. After that, near-zero upkeep through the weekly routine.

## 3. Paid Content Review Panel

**Why.** Respected brands rest on credentials people can check. BLIND-SPOTS #10 already plans a one-time accuracy review by a CCC-SLP. Turning it into a small standing panel gives a faceless brand checkable credentials. It does this without implying the founder is an SLP or that any product is therapy.

**The rules that apply**
- **FTC 16 CFR 255.3(b)**, a long-standing rule and not new in 2023: an expert endorsement must reflect a real exercise of that expertise, at least as thorough as a peer would need.
- **The 2023 revision** restated that endorsers can be liable for their own statements (255.1(d)) and that payment must be disclosed (255.5). That is why the credit line says reviewers are paid.
- **ASHA Code of Ethics (2023), Principle III:** no misrepresenting products or their effects, and avoid conflicts of interest.

**What to do**
- **Panel:** a CCC-SLP, a pediatric OT and an early-childhood educator. Each product gets **one primary reviewer by topic**:
  - SLP: talk tips.
  - OT: fine-motor and sensory play, and the safety notes.
  - Educator: classroom and group kits.
- **Recruit in writing only**, through freelance marketplaces and state speech-language-hearing association job boards. Use legal/protection/freelancer-work-for-hire-and-ip-assignment.md.
- **Written scope:**
  - (a) Accuracy and child-safety review of general parent-education content only.
  - (b) No therapy, no assessment, and no claim that the product works.
  - (c) The reviewer did not write the material.
  - (d) The reviewer approves the exact credit line: *"Reviewed for accuracy by [Name], M.S., CCC-SLP. Reviewers are paid for their time and did not write this material. This is parent education, not therapy."*
  - (e) Re-review whenever the product changes.
- **Before listing anyone:**
  - Verify their state licence and ASHA certification, and link to both lookups from their bio.
  - Run them through the CLAUDE.md exclusion list.
  - Do not hire anyone the founder knows through work (FOR-EMPLOYMENT-COUNSEL question 10).
- **Where the name may appear:** an inside page and the website only.
  - **Never** on a cover, in KDP contributor fields, in marketplace keywords, in ads or on badges.
  - No "SLP-approved" or "expert-approved" seals (TRUST-CHECKLIST "Never" list).
- **Log each review in git:** date, file version, reviewer and changes made.

**Budget.** Rates are aggregator snapshots [VERIFY]:
- Freelance SLP about $44/hr ($36–52).
- Medical reviewers $22–72/hr.
- Sensitivity reads $0.013–0.04 per word.

Corrected maths: a full three-reviewer pass on 13 products would cost about $5,850–15,600. That is why each product gets one primary reviewer.

**First action:** post a written brief for the SLP only, then add the others as revenue allows. **Cost:**
- $150–400 flat per product: about **$1,200–3,200 for the 8 current products** (about $1,950–5,200 for 13).
- Starting option: an SLP retainer of **$1,200–2,400 a year** for quarterly checks.
- Founder time: about 6–10 hours of approvals in total.

## 4. "Virtual autism": explain it once, then don't use it

**Why.** The brand already agrees with the critics. Section 4 of marketing/virtual-autism-outreach.md says the term is not a diagnosis, that the studies show associations and not causes, and that the parent-blame criticism is fair: "The first three points are correct, and we agree with them." Yet content/founder-story.md still uses the term in its short and long versions, the outreach bio reuses it, and index.html still shows "The Virtual Autism Project". For a brand that wants respect, the term should move from "allowed with framing" to "explained once, then not used".

**The history.** Parent blame has a documented history: Waltz M., "Mothers and Autism: The Evolution of a Discourse of Blame", *AMA Journal of Ethics* 2015;17(4):353–358. This is the correct source. The ASAN 2017 piece is not; it covers the politics of neurodiversity, causation and cure.

**The 2026 paper.** A peer-reviewed commentary reportedly makes the same case: Krijnen et al., "Why the Term 'Virtual Autism' Warrants Caution", *Autism*, March 2026, doi 10.1177/13623613261434478. It is **[VERIFY]**: the verifier had no independent record of it. Do not cite it until someone opens it and the founder adds it to rule 5.

**What to do (proposal for the founder)**
- **Tighter rule 3.** The phrase appears in exactly one research-hub explainer, "Why we don't use the term 'virtual autism'". That page:
  - says the term is not a diagnosis;
  - says the studies show associations;
  - names the parent-blame history, citing the AMA piece only after it is on the rule-5 list or marked [VERIFY].
- **Neutral wording everywhere else:** "less talk and play time", "screen time and early language". Avoid "screen-related developmental delay" too, because "delay" is clinical language.
- **Founder story and outreach bio:** switch to the cut version that leaves the term out. Finish renaming the hub in index.html.
- **Keep "autism" out** of all product copy, SEO and ads. This is already binding.
- **Sensitivity read:** 1–2 paid autistic sensitivity readers check the explainer before it goes live. This is already budgeted in BLIND-SPOTS #10.

**First action:** put the rule-3 proposal in ops/APPROVALS.md and swap the founder-story version. **Cost:** 2–3 hours of copy edits, plus a sensitivity read ($150–600). Checking the paper takes about 15 minutes in a session with web access.

## 5. Representation and home-language standard

**Why.** A talk-focused brand earns respect from disability communities and multilingual families in two ways: its pictures include children who communicate differently, and its tips tell parents to use their home language. ASHA's bilingual guidance says learning two languages does not cause speech or language disorders, and advises families to use the language they know best. Nationwide Children's (2017) says the same. CCBC data has long shown disability in only a small share of children's books. The earlier percentages contradicted each other, so **quote no CCBC figure** until it is checked. ASHA is not on the rule-5 list, so any public citation of it needs [VERIFY] or a rule-5 addition. The home-language tip itself needs no citation.

**What to do (cast rule proposed for BRAND.md)**
- **Across each series, include:** children who sign, point, or use a picture board or AAC device, plus glasses, hearing aids, a wheelchair and a limb difference. They simply take part: never a lesson, never an outcome caption. Draw devices generically, with no real product shapes (rule 2).
- **Families:** varied family structures, with dads and grandparents shown as everyday main caregivers.
- **Cast bible:** named SVG symbols with fixed skin, hair and assistive-device details, so the cast stays consistent across books.
- **Standard line on every talk-tip product:** *"Talk, sing and read in the language you know best. Every language counts."*
- **"Say the word" tips:** rewrite them so a sign, a point or a device tap also counts as communicating.
- **Languages:** Spanish printable editions first, then a blank "your language" version where families write the words in themselves.
- **Sensitivity reads:** pay an AAC-user or autistic reader, and a disability reader, to check the board-book cast.

**First action:** the founder approves the cast rule; the home-language line goes into the shared tip templates. **Cost:** design time, plus $300–600 per sensitivity read. Spanish translation with professional review is about $0.10–0.20 per word [VERIFY].

## 6. Accessibility and colour-contrast standard

**Why.** Accessibility is a cheap, visible respect signal, and increasingly a school purchasing requirement. The brand palette fails in ways we can measure (WCAG luminance and a Machado 2009 colour-vision simulation, computed this pass):

| Pair | Contrast | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Tomato vs grass | 1.07:1 | Almost identical lightness; both look olive or khaki to red-green colour-blind readers |
| White on tomato | 3.41:1 | Fails body text |
| White on grass | 3.20:1 | Fails body text |
| White on sky | 3.75:1 | Fails body text |
| White on sun | 1.79:1 | Fails even the large-text minimum |
| Ink on tomato | 4.26:1 | Fails body text |
| Ink on grass | 4.55:1 | Passes |
| Ink on sun | 8.15:1 | Passes |
| Plum on white | 4.73:1 | Passes |

**The legal picture**
- **EU Accessibility Act:** applies since June 28, 2025 to ebooks and e-commerce sold to EU consumers. Microenterprises that provide services (fewer than 10 staff, €2M or less) are exempt, and ebooks count as services. The harmonized standard EN 301 549 incorporates WCAG 2.1 AA. EPUB Accessibility 1.1 is the ebook conformance spec.
- **US, DOJ 2024 ADA Title II rule:** public school districts must meet WCAG 2.1 AA from April 24, 2026 (population of 50,000 or more) or April 26, 2027 (smaller) [VERIFY current status]. Schools that post our files on a website or learning platform will need accessible files.

**What to do**
- Meet **WCAG 2.1 AA** on the site anyway.
- Make **tagged PDFs the default**, not "on request" as TRUST-CHECKLIST #28 says now. Offer accessible EPUB and large print where the format allows.
- **Colour rules, proposed for BRAND.md:**
  1. White body text only on ink or plum.
  2. White on tomato, sky or grass only at large sizes (24px regular, or 18.66px bold, and up).
  3. Never white on sun; use ink on sun.
  4. Never let tomato versus grass alone carry meaning (do/don't, ready/not ready); add an icon or a label.
- Add a **contrast check** (body text at least 4.5:1) to the render.js QA pass. COMPLIANCE-GATE #20 already requires the result.
- Prepare a **one-page accessibility conformance summary** for the classroom pack.

**First action:** add the contrast check to render.js. **Cost:** about 4 hours once, 1–2 hours per product for tagging, and about 2 hours for the conformance summary.

## 7. Reviews-and-criticism policy

**Why.** Criticism of a faceless brand happens in reviews and comments. Respected brands answer once, fix the substance and show the fix. The current reply model covers only "virtual autism" (virtual-autism-outreach section 4).

**The law** (the earlier citation to the Green Guides was wrong):
- **FTC Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465**, effective October 21, 2024. It bans:
  - incentives conditioned on a review's sentiment (465.4);
  - suppressing reviews through threats, or presenting displayed reviews as all reviews (465.7);
  - fake or undisclosed insider reviews.
- **Consumer Review Fairness Act (15 U.S.C. 45b):** voids form-contract terms that restrict honest reviews.

**What to do: write operations/REVIEWS-AND-CRITICISM.md**
- **Saved replies for 1–3 star reviews:** thank the reviewer, name the specific fix or refund route, and link to the corrections log.
- **Where a platform allows no seller reply:** fix and log instead. Some book marketplaces allow no public replies [VERIFY per platform].
- **Never** condition a refund, replacement or anything else on removing or changing a review.
- **Confirm** that the Terms of Use and the digital-product licence contain no non-disparagement or review-restriction clause.
- **Never** ask fans to respond.
- **Monthly digest:** the routine lists the top 3 recurring complaints. They feed product revisions and the "You asked, we changed" section of the editorial page.
- **Founder approval:** only for non-template replies, in the weekly check.

**First action:** write the file and check the Terms of Use. **Cost:** about 3 hours once; the routine runs it after that.

## 8. Versioned files and a safety-correction runbook

**Why.** Respected brands handle mistakes, especially safety mistakes, by telling every buyer and replacing for free. Lovevery's 2022 cup recall and 2024 Slide & Seek Ball Run recall came with free replacements. That is internal reference only; never name them publicly.
- **CPSA §15(b):** a report to CPSC is required within 24 hours of information that reasonably supports a conclusion that a product has a defect that could create a substantial hazard.
- **Card decks:** a physical deck meant for children under 12 is a children's product under CPSIA. That means third-party testing, a Children's Product Certificate and tracking labels (BLIND-SPOTS #9).
- **Store updates:** stores do not all resend updated files automatically, and a KDP interior update never reaches past print buyers [VERIFY per platform].

**What to do**
- Put **"Version x.y · Month Year"** in the footer of every PDF and POD interior. None of the 8 current products has one.
- **Digital updates:** use each store's notify-buyers feature where it exists. Otherwise the routine emails past buyers from the order export with a one-line "what changed" note. Never swap a file quietly.
- **Write ops/SAFETY-CORRECTION.md** for any safety-relevant error:
  1. Unlist the product the same day.
  2. Email all buyers a plain explanation and a fixed file or a free POD replacement.
  3. Post it on the corrections log.
  4. Have counsel assess the 24-hour CPSC reporting question.
- Safety issues skip the weekly batch and go to the founder immediately. This is the one exception to "zero daily tasks".

**First action:** add the version footer to the shared print template. **Cost:** 2–3 hours once. Replacement costs only when needed.

## 9. Giving: a fixed annual book gift, never tied to sales

**Why.** State charity laws can turn a "portion of proceeds" or "every purchase gives" promise into a regulated charitable sales promotion.
- **Commercial co-venturer laws:** about two dozen states regulate them. Alabama, Hawaii, Illinois, Maine, Massachusetts and South Carolina require advance registration. Several states require a filed contract, a final accounting and exact ad disclosures.
- **"Organization or purpose":** under the model act, the rule covers a claim that a purchase benefits a charitable organization **or purpose**, so leaving the charity unnamed does not escape it.
- **Tax:** IRC 170(e)(1)(A) limits donations of inventory and a creator's own work to cost basis. The enhanced inventory deduction is for C-corporations. A single-member LLC that has already expensed its print costs cannot deduct them a second time.
- **Dropped from the earlier pass:** the Reach Out and Read NYC / Barefoot item describes books bought for programs, not a publisher donation, so it is not a model. CAMPAIGN-BIBLE already cut the PTA "Pass It Down" meter.

**What to do**
- **Never** make a per-sale or "every purchase gives" claim, whether or not a charity is named.
- **(a) A fixed annual commitment:** *"Each year AlphaPlay LLC gives printed books to early-literacy and child-care programs."*
  - Recipients apply through a written form; we do no outreach.
  - POD copies ship straight to the program.
  - Every applicant is checked against the CLAUDE.md exclusion list. The routine never replies to an excluded body and holds it for the founder.
- **(b) The Community Licence** (move 10), presented as a standing offer.
- **Accounting:** the CPA decides whether print cost is booked as a marketing expense or as a charitable deduction at cost, never both. Log it as [VERIFY-CPA] in finance/.
- **Report the gift:** a one-line annual "What we gave" figure (copies, programs, print cost) on the About page, computed by the routine from the donation log. This also fulfils TRUST-CHECKLIST #47.
- **Reference:** First Book's eligibility test (a Title I school, or at least 70% of children from low-income families) is a good model for the application form.

**First action:** send the CPA question; draft the application form. **Cost:** it scales. About **$700–1,000 a year** for 100 POD paperbacks ($4–6 print cost each, plus shipping). No registration or bond is needed while giving stays untied from sales.

## 11. "Play Testers" email panel

**Why.** The category leader's "expert-backed" reputation rests on large-scale family testing as much as on experts. Lovevery reportedly cites 600+ families and 30+ touchpoints per product [VERIFY; internal only]. We can run the same thing by email as the ongoing arm of BLIND-SPOTS #13–#14.

**What to do**
- **Joining:** parents join by email and give only the child's age band, never a name.
- **Monthly round:** 3–5 draft printables go to 20–40 testers with a 6-question form:
  1. Did the child engage?
  2. How many minutes did they play?
  3. Which tip was confusing?
  4. What would you change?
  5. May we quote you by role or first initial?
  6. Do you consent to share photos? (This uses legal/protection/model-release-minor.md.)
- **Payment:** a free finished product. Remind testers in writing to disclose it if they post a review (16 CFR 255.5, 465).
- **Where data lives:**
  - Signed consents and survey exports stay in the form tool or secure storage, **never in git**.
  - Only anonymized counts go into git.
  - Claim "tested by N families" only when that log supports N.
- **Never** use quotes about speech, development or behaviour results (rule 1).
- **Honest line for guides:** *"Most children love 2–3 of these; that's normal."*

**First action:** build the form and the count log. **Cost:** $0–200 a month in free products. Fully async.
