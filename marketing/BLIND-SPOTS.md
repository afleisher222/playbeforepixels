## When revenue starts

**16. Pay for the board-book print run with a pre-sale.**
- **Why:** A pre-sale pays for the print run, tells you how many to print, grows your list and gets early copies to reviewers. Rebel Girls funded its first book on Kickstarter [VERIFY].
- **First step:** Once the quotes are in, set the goal: printing + shipping + duties + about 8–10% fees + delivery to buyers + a 15% buffer. Then plan a Feb–Apr 2027 pre-sale, ideally around spring break.
  - Use Shopify pre-orders or Kickstarter. Kickstarter shows the creator's verified name [VERIFY], so ask counsel about that.
  - Tiers: 1 book; the 3-book set plus a printable; 10 copies donated to a library or child-care center; bulk 25/100/500.
  - Order what sold plus 30–50%.
  - Ship by the date you promised, or tell buyers and offer a refund. The FTC mail-order rule requires this.
- **Time:** 20–40 hours around the campaign.

**17. Hire a human illustrator for the print-run books, and use one contract for every freelancer.**
- **Why:** Human-made art can be protected by copyright, and a "play before pixels" audience may not like AI-drawn children's books. A clean contract means the business owns what it pays for.
- **First step:** Before the board-book print order, send 2–3 illustrators the AI drafts as layout guides.
  - The contract should cover: the business owns the work, with a backup transfer of rights; the artist discloses any AI use; you get the source files; the artist may show the work in a portfolio.
  - Use the same template for the SLP, sensitivity readers and any helper: ownership goes to AlphaPlay LLC, plus confidentiality.
  - Keep a list of your font and stock licenses.
- **Cost:** About $1,500–$5,000 per board book. Having a lawyer check the template costs $300–$700.

**18. Build library credibility with one paid review and a few awards.**
- **Why:** Trade journals rarely review self-published books. A reputable paid review and juried awards are the realistic way onto library shelves.
- **First step:** For the lead title's traditionally printed edition:
  - add a library cataloging block to the copyright page;
  - make it orderable through IngramSpark;
  - buy one Kirkus Indie or Foreword Clarion review;
  - enter 2–3 juried awards (Foreword INDIES, IPPY, Moonbeam), and skip any contest that sells award seals.
- **Cost:** Cataloging block about $75–$150. Review about $500–$650. Awards about $75–$100 each [VERIFY].

**19. Make it easy for groups to buy, and put fundraiser terms in writing, once counsel clears it.** FLAG FOR EMPLOYMENT COUNSEL ONLY
- **Why:** PTAs, libraries and child-care centers rarely pay by card. They need a quote, a W-9 and an invoice payable in 30 days. "A portion benefits your PTA" can also trigger state charity-sales registration, as in Massachusetts [VERIFY].
- **First step:** After counsel clears sales to groups, build:
  - a Groups page with a quote form;
  - a price sheet with 25/100/500 tiers and personal, classroom and organization licenses;
  - invoices through Shopify;
  - an organization license certificate;
  - a W-9 sent privately on request;
  - the FAQ line "Not approved for state training credit."

  Have the attorney check a fundraiser agreement that states the exact dollars per item.
- **FLAG FOR EMPLOYMENT COUNSEL ONLY:** A do-not-contact list, and clearance before any quote, pitch or sale to your employer, its schools, or connected PTAs and associations.

**20. Make sure the business can keep running if you can't.**
- **Why:** A one-owner LLC with no named backup stalls if you're unavailable, which leaves royalties, copyrights and deadlines out of reach. Payment holds can also freeze income for weeks.
- **First step:** Ask an attorney to add a successor to the LLC operating agreement and to draw up a durable power of attorney covering business and digital accounts. Then build a cash cushion of 2–3 months of expenses, export the list and sales every month, and write a one-page "if I'm unavailable" sheet.
- **Cost:** About $500–$1,500.
