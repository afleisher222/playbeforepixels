# 12 illustrated-post captions (faceless)

**Format:** illustrated carousels or single images in the brand style: flat vector, brand palette, no children's faces from real photos, and no founder. They can be reused as Pinterest pin descriptions; put the keyword first there.

**Before posting, check each one:**
- No health, speech or development claims.
- No "autism", diagnosis or condition hashtags.
- No naming or criticizing any app, device, school, district or company.
- No personal-attribute copy ("Is your child…?").
- Child-safety rules met in the illustration.
- The link has UTM tags.
- The routine posts only after approval.

The **hashtag bank** is safe to rotate: #screenfreeplay #playideas #toddleractivities #familytime #talkalong #quiettime #rainydayplay #kidsactivities #playbeforepixels. Never use autism, diagnosis or condition tags.

**1. "Say What You See" (0–5)**
*Illustration: a grown-up and child on a walk; speech bubbles grow "truck → big truck → big red truck".*
> Two-minute play for any walk or car ride: name one thing you see, then add one word. Truck. Big truck. Big RED truck. 🚚
> More free plays for busy days → link in bio.

**2. "The 5 p.m. basket" (0–5)**
*Illustration: a small basket holding board books and chunky blocks, with a clock showing 5:00.*
> The 5 p.m. basket: a few books and safe toys that only come out at dinner-prep time, so they always feel new. Swap the contents every Sunday.
> Save this for tonight. ✨

**3. "Restaurant plays" (carousel, 0–5)**
*Illustration: 5 slides; a napkin, sugar packets (for older kids only), a crayon and a menu become games.*
> Waiting for food? 5 plays using what's already on the table: I Spy colors, napkin peekaboo, "what's on the menu?" story, crayon trade, finger count. (Keep small items away from under-3s.)
> Printable version in our free pack → link in bio.

**4. "Talk-along reading" (0–5)**
*Illustration: an open picture book with a speech bubble on each side.*
> Turn any picture book into a conversation: pause and wait, point and name, repeat and add one word. There's no right way. Just take turns.

**5. "Sock basketball" (2–8)**
*Illustration: rolled socks arcing into a laundry basket, with a big number counter.*
> Rainy-day rescue: roll socks into balls, toss them into the laundry basket, count out loud. Move the basket back one step every round. 🧦🏀

**6. "Family screen agreement" (5–12)**
*Illustration: a fridge with a one-page family plan held on by magnets.*
> For families with school-age kids: a one-page agreement everyone helps write works better than a rule handed down. Start with 3 questions: When? Where? What do we do instead?
> Free starter template → link in bio.

**7. "Story in three" (4–10)**
*Illustration: three speech bubbles numbered 1, 2, 3 around a dinner table.*
> Dinner-table game: you say the first sentence of a story, they say the second, you all say the ending. Silliest ending wins. 🍝

**8. "Car ride plays" (carousel, 3–10)**
*Illustration: a car window view; license plates and clouds become game prompts.*
> Car ride with no screen? Try: alphabet signs, cloud shapes, "20 questions: animal edition", the humming game, and "what will we see next?" 🚗
> All 10 car plays in the free printable.

**9. "Quiet time" (2–5)**
*Illustration: a reading nook with a pillow, a lamp and a stack of books.*
> Quiet time doesn't need to be silent. Set up a cozy corner with books, a pillow and one "quiet" activity. Same place, same time, every day.

**11. "7-Day Screen-Free Challenge" (seasonal: New Year / early May)**
*Illustration: a 7-square calendar, each square with a small icon (ball, book, blocks, puzzle, crayons, music note, sun).*
