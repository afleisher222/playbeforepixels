**300-word newsletter column: "Five plays for the 5 p.m. slump"**
> Late afternoon is hard for everyone. Here are five plays that take two minutes to start and need nothing you don't already have.
>
> 1. **Kitchen helper:** give your child a job with you: washing vegetables, stirring, counting spoons. Talk about what you're both doing.
> 2. **Sock basketball:** roll socks into balls and toss them into a laundry basket. Count out loud together. For babies and toddlers, use soft, large items only, with an adult close by.
> 3. **Say what you see:** name things you notice, then add one word: "Truck." "Big truck." "Big red truck."
> 4. **Story in three:** you say one sentence of a story, your child says the next, and you finish it together.
> 5. **Quiet basket:** keep a small basket of books and safe, age-appropriate toys that only comes out at 5 p.m., so it always feels new.
>
> Studies have looked at how family routines and back-and-forth conversation fit into young children's days.
