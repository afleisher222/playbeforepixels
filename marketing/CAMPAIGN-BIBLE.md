# Play Before Pixels: Campaign Bible

Status: draft for founder approval, September 28, 2026. Owner: AlphaPlay LLC, doing business as Play Before Pixels.

This file turns the 40 scored campaign ideas into one plan: how we make screen-free feel cool, the top 20 campaigns, a 12-month calendar, the Virtual Autism Education Series, the new products these campaigns need, and what we cut.

**Binding sources, in this order:** `brand/BRAND.md` (hard rules win every conflict), `marketing/BLIND-SPOTS.md` (counsel hold and launch waves), `marketing/DEMAND-CHECK.md` (prices and what sells), `marketing/virtual-autism-outreach.md` (who we never contact about the term). Where an idea's original price or product conflicts with DEMAND-CHECK, this bible uses the DEMAND-CHECK figure and says so.

**How the top 20 was chosen.** Three judges scored every idea from 0 to 10: Coolness and Shareability (C), Education Value and Trust (E), and Revenue and Self-Running (R). The total is C + E + R, out of 30. Where totals tie, the higher R score ranks first, because a self-running business is binding. If R also ties, the higher E score ranks first. Five ideas tie at 17.5 for places 16 to 20 and all five made the list. The next idea scored 17.0.

## 1. The "cool" positioning and voice guide

### Positioning (one paragraph)

Play Before Pixels makes the unplugged hours the best part of the day, not the leftover part. For kids 5 to 12, screen-free means status you can hold in your hand: a Guild rank, a cracked code, a Legendary quest card, a cardboard bridge that held 25 books, a party where every guest leaves as a certified agent. For babies and toddlers, it means belonging for the grown-ups: a proud talk-first club where every babble, point, sign and giggle counts as a turn, and the wait for dinner turns into a game. Our mascot is a friendly sleepy tablet in a nightcap. It is not the villain. It just takes a nap while the adventure happens: **"The tablet's napping. The world is open."** We never tell anyone that screens are bad or that a family is failing. Families at every level of screen use are welcome, and on sick days, flights and hard weeks, rest comes first. We are honest about the research: studies show links, not proof of cause, and we say so every time. Screen-free is sold as freedom, adventure, making things, belonging and pride, never as guilt, fear or being better than anyone else.

### Voice guide

**Tone in five words:** warm, plain, playful, proud, honest.

**Words and phrases we use**

| Theme | Use |
|---|---|
| Adventure and status (5–12) | quest, mission, unlock, rank, Rookie, Trailblazer, Captain, Legend, agent, code name, crew, Guild, dispatch, Legendary, collect them all, personal best, "beat your own record" |
| Making | build, make, invent, test, design, blueprint, tinker, "built by me" |
| Talk (0–5) | back-and-forth, turn, rally, "every way of talking counts" (words, babbles, signs, gestures, pictures, AAC) |
| Talk strategies (plain words only) | pause and wait, say what you see, repeat and add one word, offer a choice, follow their lead, sing and gesture |
| Screens | the tablet's napping; tuck it in; screen-optional; a rest day for the tablet; "when you're ready"; "at your family's pace" |
| Research | linked with; associated with; one study found / one study estimated; a link, not proof of cause; what it can't tell us; a guideline advises |
| Worry | "Talk with your pediatrician"; "free early intervention evaluation"; "asking early is a caring step"; "it's not your fault" |
| People | grown-up, family, caregiver, grandparent; autistic people (identity-first where people prefer it, and we ask) |
| Safety | "with your grown-up"; "grown-up nearby"; "for ages 3+" |

**Words and phrases we never use**

| Never | Why |
|---|---|
| addict, addiction, hooked, zombie, brain rot, toxic, poison, drug comparisons, "melting their brains" | fear and shame |
| detox, cold turkey, ban, "no-screen rule you'll fail" | loss and punishment framing |
| "real life" as the opposite of screen life, "real kids", "better parents", lazy, guilt, fail, "screen moment" (about a family's choice) | moral superiority; implies some families are lesser |
| nicknames that label kids by a device or brand; any real show, creator, app, device brand, school, district or EdTech company | brand rule 2 |
| cure, treat, heal, reverse, prevent (about autism or delay), therapy, speech therapy, clinically proven, intervention (as something we sell) | brand rule 1; no health claims |
| "screens cause autism", "virtual autism" on anything outside the research hub, "autism epidemic", "fighting autism", "battle", "suffers from autism", "tragedy" | brand rule 3 and respect for autistic people |
| puzzle pieces, sirens, alarm red, warning triangles, crying-child imagery | fear imagery |
| milestone (in play content), "on track", "behind", score, test (about a child) | our play pages are not developmental checklists |
| Scout, merit badge, sash, "-a-thon", "rescue" | other programs' trade dress and names |
| SLP, speech therapist, clinician (about the founder or the brand) | brand rule 1; no implied credentials |

**Three voice checks before anything ships**
1. Would a parent who used a tablet at dinner last night feel welcome reading this? If not, rewrite it.
2. Would an autistic adult feel respected reading this? If not, rewrite it.
3. Is every research line either an allowed citation stated as an association, or marked [VERIFY]?

## Standing rules used in every campaign below

These short codes are referenced in section 2 and section 5 so each entry doesn't repeat them.

- **[G0] Launch now.** Parent-facing products for ages 0–5, research-hub pages and free lead magnets.
- **[G1] Parent products for ages 5–12.** BLIND-SPOTS #1 says to launch only 0–5 products until employment counsel answers. The playbook says "parent products, 0–5 first." Default: build now, publish only when the counsel answer allows it. Every [G1] calendar slot names a [G0] fallback.
- **[G2] School, child-care, library, PTA or group-facing.** Built but unpublished until counsel clears school- and group-facing work (BLIND-SPOTS #1; playbook gate). Nothing the founder made for or used in her own teaching may be sold. All group sales run by written quote form, emailed invoice and PO.
- **[UGC] Customer content rule.** Adults submit by form with a signed release. Hands, objects and paper only: no faces, names (including names written on posters or handwriting that shows a full name), voices saying names, school logos, locations, boarding passes or restaurant details. Metadata stripped. No contests or prizes.
- **[COPPA] Parent-only data.** Every signup is adult-only with an "I am the parent or guardian and 18+" tick. We collect no child name, age, birthday, photo or other child data online; birth month and year only, from the adult, and only where BRAND.md allows. Emails go to grown-ups. Kid-facing pages (answer pages, QR landings) have no forms, ads, third-party trackers or analytics cookies. QR codes on children's items are labelled "For grown-ups".
- **[CPSIA] Children's-product gate.** Any physical item marketed to children 12 and under (patches, youth apparel, kids' sticker sheets, kids' card decks, kid aprons) is listed only if the POD partner supplies a Children's Product Certificate and tracking labels [VERIFY per item]. Until then, sell the printable version or an adult version. DEMAND-CHECK already cuts kids' tees and makes stickers bundle-only.
- **[SAFE] Rule 4 safety line.** Every activity is reviewed against BRAND.md rule 4: adult supervision; nothing that fits through a toilet-paper tube for under-3s; no balloons for under-8s; no cords or strings long enough to wrap a neck; supervised water play; no whole grapes, nuts, popcorn or hard candy. Each product carries its own printed safety line.
- **[CITE] Research wording.** Allowed citations only (BRAND.md rule 5). Brushe 2024 is always "one study estimated that, at 36 months, higher screen time was linked with about 1,139 fewer adult words, 843 fewer child vocalizations and 194 fewer back-and-forth turns a day; it shows a link, not proof of cause" [VERIFY comparison wording against the paper]. Delgado 2018 is always "a review of 54 studies with 171,000+ readers found a modest comprehension advantage for reading on paper, mostly for informational texts" [VERIFY the informational-text and timed-reading nuance]. WHO 2019 is always "the WHO guideline advises…" [VERIFY WHO's own evidence-certainty wording before calling anything settled].
- **[HUB] Autism-term isolation.** The term "virtual autism" appears only in research-hub material, framed with the safe sentence in section 4. It never appears on merch, in children's books, in kid-facing kits, in listings, ads, hashtags or product emails. No product, price or mascot sits beside it. Never boosted, never an ad hook, never tagged #autism, and always through the weekly approval queue.
- **[LIST] Listing standard.** Every product follows BRAND.md deliverables: `products/<slug>/` with source.html, PDF, preview PNGs, cover.png, mockup.png and listing.json including `next_products` and `bonus_url`; a QR and short link to a free bonus; marked slots for the founder's own creative contribution (human authorship); a note to check the printer's current template; the AlphaPlay LLC copyright line.
- **Prices.** Etsy listings use an anchor list price and run at 30–40% off (DEMAND-CHECK rule 2). No single page is listed below $5. Physical POD versions launch only after the printable version sells (DEMAND-CHECK rule 8).

### 1. The Back-and-Forth Tally (home challenge and child-care center edition) — 23.0

- **One-liner:** A 7-day "rally record" game in which grown-ups count back-and-forth talk turns in 10 minutes of play and try to beat their own best.
- **How it works:** A parent signs up and gets a one-page tally sheet styled like a ping-pong scoreboard. Each day they play for 10 minutes and make a mark for each back-and-forth: the child babbles, points, signs, gestures, uses words or an AAC device; the grown-up responds; the child responds again. Every way of communicating counts. If counting during play gets in the way, parents tally right afterward from memory. Seven automated emails each teach one plain-words strategy with one gentle research note ([CITE] Brushe wording, then "The good news: turns are easy to add"). Day 7 brings a printable "New Rally Record" certificate. Scoring is only against your own last week. Center edition: staff tally cards for routines (diapering, snack, outdoors), EN/ES family fridge cards, five strategy posters, a lobby Tally Wall and a Friday grand-total certificate. Every sheet says "This is a play game, not a measurement or screening tool" and "Worried about development? Talk to your pediatrician and ask about free early intervention."
- **Audience:** parents of babies to 5-year-olds; later, child-care centers, preschools, home providers and family resource programs.
- **Education angle:** parents learn what a conversational turn is by counting them, which sticks better than reading about it. **Cool angle:** a personal-best scoreboard and a certificate worth posting.
- **Product tie-in and price:** home challenge free (main 0–5 lead magnet). The Day 7 upsell points to the Talk Tower game kit printable ($6.99; it replaces the cut "More Talk, Less Tap" book per DEMAND-CHECK) and 100 Plays Before Pixels ($16.99 paperback / $9.99 PDF), not the cut book. A 30-day "Rally Season" is part of the 30-Day Screen Reset ($27; $49 bundle, per DEMAND-CHECK). Center kit $39; multi-site license $129 [G2].
- **Runs automatically:** email-platform sequence; fillable PDF certificate; center licenses delivered by automated email with a stamped PDF; a weekly routine reports signup-to-purchase conversion into the Friday scorecard.
- **What to build:** tally sheet (Letter and A4), 7 emails, certificate, a "what counts as a turn" carousel showing words, signs, gestures and AAC, center kit files (EN/ES), stamping script, `products/back-and-forth-tally/`. [UGC] for certificate photos. No trademarked program names and no turn-counting technology products mentioned.
- **Effort:** S home edition; M center edition.

## 4. The Virtual Autism Education Series

If you're worried about your child, talk with your pediatrician and ask about a free early intervention evaluation.
