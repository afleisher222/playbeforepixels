# Screen-free brain breaks for the classroom

A brain break doesn't need a video, a projector or a login. All it needs is **you, your voice and 60 to 180 seconds.** The 30 breaks below need no prep and no materials beyond what's already in your room, and every one has a seated or low-movement version so every student can join in.

> **Classroom safety:** clear a space before active breaks, set a "freeze" signal, and keep movement in place when the room is tight. No balloons (a choking hazard for under-8s). In PreK rooms, every object must be too big to fit through a toilet-paper tube. Always offer a seated option. Let students opt for the calm version without having to explain why.

## How to run a great brain break

1. **Signal it.** Use the same chime, clap or phrase every time: "Brain break in three, two, one…"
2. **Model it.** Do the move yourself first. Students copy you far more readily than they follow instructions.
3. **Keep it short.** One to three minutes. Stop while it's still fun.
4. **Close it.** End with a calm cue, such as three slow breaths or "hands on desks," so everyone returns ready to learn.

## Energizers (when the room is sluggish)

| # | Break | How | Seated version |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Freeze dance, teacher-sung** | Sing or hum; everyone freezes when you stop. | Freeze with arms and faces only. |
| 2 | **Count and clap** | Count to 20. Clap on every multiple of 2, then 3, then 5. | Same, from seats. |
| 3 | **Simon says** | Classic, but nobody is out. Everyone keeps playing. | Arm, face and hand moves only. |
| 4 | **Weather report** | "Drizzle" (finger taps), "rain" (pats), "thunder" (stomps), "sunshine" (arms up). | Taps on the desk instead of stomps. |
| 5 | **Mirror me** | Partners face each other; one leads slow moves, the other mirrors. Swap. | Hands and faces only. |
| 6 | **Jump the number** | Call out a number; students do that many jumps, claps or waves. | Claps or waves. |
| 7 | **Animal parade** | Name an animal; everyone moves like it in place. | Make the animal's face and "paws." |
| 8 | **Shake it out** | Shake right hand 8, left 8, right foot 8, left foot 8, then 4s, 2s, 1s. | Hands only. |
| 9 | **Would you rather: stand or sit** | "Would you rather be a fish (stand) or a bird (sit)?" | Hands up or hands down. |
| 10 | **Silent ball** | Pass a soft ball in silence; a drop means a gentle sit. | Pass only to neighbors. |

## Calmers (after recess, before tests, when it's loud)

| # | Break | How | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | **Five-finger breathing** | Trace up a finger breathing in, down it breathing out, for all five fingers. | Works anywhere. |
| 12 | **Balloon belly** (imaginary) | Hands on belly: breathe in to "fill the balloon," out to let it go. | No real balloons. |
| 13 | **Candle and flower** | Smell the flower (in), blow out the candle (out). Repeat five times. | Great for PreK. |
| 14 | **Listening minute** | Eyes closed, if comfortable; count every sound you hear. | Share the count after. |
| 15 | **Slow-motion walk** | Walk to the door and back in the slowest motion possible. | Seated: slow-motion arm wave. |
| 16 | **Stretch and name** | Reach high: "sun." Reach wide: "rainbow." Fold down: "seed." | Seated stretches. |
| 17 | **Squeeze and release** | Squeeze fists for 5 seconds, then let go. Then shoulders, then toes. | Fully seated. |
| 18 | **Quiet counting** | Count backward from 20 together, getting quieter each number. | |
| 19 | **Draw your breath** | Draw a wave on paper that rises as you breathe in and falls as you breathe out. | Uses scrap paper. |
| 20 | **Gratitude whisper** | Whisper one good thing to a neighbor, or to yourself. | |

## Thinkers (movement plus a quick brain challenge)

| # | Break | How |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | **Spell and move** | Spell a word: arms up for tall letters, hands on hips for short ones, touch toes for letters that drop below the line. |
| 22 | **Four corners** | Label the room's corners A–D and ask a fun question. Students walk to their answer. |
| 23 | **Skip-count stretch** | Skip-count by 2s, 5s or 10s, stretching a different way for each number. |
| 24 | **Opposites** | You say "up"; they do "down." You say "fast"; they do "slow." |
| 25 | **Category clap** | Name a category (fruits, shapes, verbs); each student adds one and claps once. |
| 26 | **Shape it** | Make a triangle, circle or square with your arms, then with a partner. |
| 27 | **Story in motion** | Tell a 30-second story; students act out each verb. |
| 28 | **Air writing** | Write spelling words or numbers in the air with an elbow, a foot or your nose. |
| 29 | **Rhyme or stand** | Say two words: stand if they rhyme, stay seated if they don't. |
| 30 | **Pass the pattern** | Start a clap-snap pattern; each student adds one beat. |

## Why screen-free?

Video brain breaks are popular and can be fun. A screen-free break adds something different: **students move with each other and with you**, not with a character on a screen. There's no loading time, no volume fiddling, no ads to skip, and it works in a power cut or on a field trip.

For early childhood rooms, the World Health Organization's 2019 guideline for children under 5 recommends plenty of active play through the day and limiting sedentary screen time. For the wider picture, UNESCO's 2023 *Global Education Monitoring Report* on technology in education argues for using technology in classrooms where it clearly improves learning. Neither source is about brain breaks specifically. They simply support choosing the low-tech option when it works just as well.

## Make it a routine

- [ ] Put a brain-break card jar on your desk and draw one at random.
- [ ] Schedule breaks at natural transitions: before lunch, after recess, midway through a long block.
- [ ] Let a "brain-break captain" choose each day.
- [ ] Send the family version home so kids can teach their grown-ups.

## Inclusion notes

- Always offer a seated or hands-only version, and never single a student out.
- Keep sound levels in mind for students who find noise hard. Calmers are a good choice after loud times.
- Invite students to lead. Many shy students shine when they get to be the "mirror" leader.

## What this does not mean

Brain breaks aren't a behavior intervention or a treatment for anything. They're a simple, friendly way to reset attention and energy. Follow your school's policies and the plans in place for individual students.
