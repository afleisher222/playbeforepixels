# What to do instead of screens with a toddler

Most of us don't hand a toddler the phone because we think it's a great idea. We reach for it because we need four minutes to drain the pasta, finish a call or survive a line at the pharmacy.

So this list is organized by **those moments**, the real reasons screens come out, with swaps that actually work for 1- to 3-year-olds. None needs a trip to the store.

> **Safety for this age:** an adult is always nearby. Nothing small enough to fit through a toilet-paper tube. No balloons. No cords or long strings. Stay within arm's reach during water play. No whole grapes, nuts, popcorn or hard candy.

## When you're cooking

1. **The toddler cupboard.** One low cupboard of plastic bowls, lids, wooden spoons and a colander. Rotate what's in it weekly so it stays interesting.
2. **"Wash" the vegetables.** Seat your toddler at a small tub of water with a large whole potato and a cloth. Skip carrots and anything else a toddler could bite a piece off. Keep the tub within arm's reach and empty it straight away.
3. **Narrate like a cooking show.** "Now I'm stirring. Stir, stir, stir! Now it's bubbling!" Your toddler's job is to say "Stir!" back.
4. **Kitchen band.** A pot and a wooden spoon. Loud, yes. Also joyful.
5. **Sort the containers.** Big ones here, small ones there. Nest them. Stack them. Knock them down.

## When you're on the phone or answering email

6. **The "only-when-I'm-busy" basket.** A small basket of things your toddler only sees during calls: a sturdy board book, a chunky puzzle, a soft toy. Novelty buys time.
7. **Scribble station.** Tape a big sheet of paper to the table or floor and offer one chunky crayon (for 2-year-olds and up, with you nearby). Put the crayon away when you're done.
8. **Posting box.** Cut a slot in a shoebox lid. Your toddler "posts" old playing cards or large jar lids, too big to fit through a toilet-paper tube.
9. **Laundry helper.** Toss socks into the basket, or pull clothes out of the dryer.

## When you're out: errands, waiting rooms, restaurants

10. **I-spy colors.** "I see something red!" Your toddler points. No right answers needed.
11. **Finger plays and songs.** The quiet version of a song with hand motions works almost anywhere.
12. **The menu game.** Point to pictures or objects and name them together: "Cup. Spoon. Big spoon!"
13. **A board book in the bag.** Always one. A new one saved just for outings is even better.
14. **Guess which hand.** Hide something you both know in one hand or behind your back, like a sock or a toy too big to fit through a toilet-paper tube.

## When everyone needs to move

15. **Box tunnel.** Open both ends of a big cardboard box.
16. **Couch-cushion path.** Cushions on the floor to climb over, with you spotting.
17. **Animal walks.** Stomp like an elephant, hop like a bunny, tiptoe like a mouse.
18. **Ball roll.** Sit facing each other with legs apart and roll a soft ball back and forth. Say "Ready… go!" and wait for your toddler to say "Go!"
19. **Bubbles.** You blow, they pop. Keep the bottle out of reach so no one drinks the solution.
20. **Outside, any weather.** Puddles, leaves, sticks, snow. Five minutes outside can reset a whole afternoon.

## When it's time to wind down

21. **Read together.** Let your toddler turn the pages, point and "read" to you.
22. **Bath-time story.** Tell a simple story about their day while you wash hair.
23. **Soft lights and a song.** Dim the lights, sing the same song every night, and it becomes a cue.
24. **Photo album on paper.** Printed family photos in a small album: "Who's that? That's Grandpa!"
25. **Cuddle and count.** Count toes, fingers or stars on the ceiling.

## The idea behind all 25

Notice what these have in common: **a grown-up nearby, a bit of talking, and your toddler doing something with their hands or body.**

The American Academy of Pediatrics' 2016 statement "Media and Young Minds" recommends avoiding screen media other than video chatting for children under 18 months, and limiting screen time for older toddlers and preschoolers. It also puts a lot of weight on hands-on play and talking together. The World Health Organization's 2019 guideline for children under 5 recommends plenty of active play and suggests reading and storytelling with a caregiver when children are sitting still.

An Australian study that recorded everyday life at home found that at 36 months, more screen time went with fewer adult words, fewer child vocalizations and fewer back-and-forth conversational turns: on average 1,139 fewer adult words, 843 fewer child sounds and words, and 194 fewer turns a day (Brushe et al., 2024). That's an **association**, not proof that screens cause less talk. But it's a helpful reminder that the little conversations in the ideas above add up.

## What this does not mean

It doesn't mean a screen during a long car ride or a sick day is a failure. Families have hard moments. The goal is to have **one or two swaps ready** for the moments that come up every day, so the screen becomes an occasional tool rather than the automatic answer.

## Three tips that make swaps work

- **Set it up before you need it.** Put the cooking basket out *before* you start cooking.
- **Start with your hardest moment.** If dinner prep is the pinch point, begin there.
- **Expect some protest at first.** It often eases as the new routine becomes familiar.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

If you have any concerns about how your toddler plays, talks, moves or responds to you, mention them to your child's doctor. You don't need to wait for a check-up. This article is general information, not medical advice.
