# Screen-free activities for every age

The best screen-free activities aren't elaborate. They use what you already have, they take a few minutes to set up, and they give your child something a screen can't: a real person to play, talk and laugh with.

Below you'll find ideas sorted by age, each with a **talk tip** (how to turn the play into conversation) and a **safety note**. Pick one that fits today. You don't need to do them all.

> **Safety first, every time.** An adult supervises every activity. For children under 3, nothing smaller than a toilet-paper tube's opening (about 1¼ inches / 3.2 cm): if it fits through, it's a choking risk. No balloons for children under 8. No cords or strings long enough to go around a neck. Stay within arm's reach during any water play. Skip choking-risk foods for toddlers, such as whole grapes, nuts, popcorn and hard candy.

## Babies: 0 to 12 months

At this age, **you are the activity.** Your face, voice and touch are the most interesting things in your baby's world.

| Activity | How | Talk tip |
|---|---|---|
| Face-to-face chat | Hold your baby about a foot from your face. Make a sound, then wait. | Pause after you speak. When your baby coos, answer as if it's a real conversation, because it is. |
| Tummy-time mirror | Lie on the floor facing your baby during tummy time. | Name what you see: "There's your nose! There's my nose!" |
| Peekaboo with a cloth | Use a small square of cloth or a napkin to hide your face. | Say "Where's Mama?" and wait a beat before "Peekaboo!" |
| Kitchen band | A wooden spoon and a pot lid, used by you or held with your baby. | "Bang, bang! Loud! Now quiet… tap, tap." |
| Narrated walks | Carry your baby around the house or outside and talk about what you see. | Say what you see: "Tree. Big tree. Leaves go swish." |

**Safety note:** stay with your baby during tummy time. Put nothing in the crib.

## Toddlers: 1 to 2 years

Toddlers love to move, copy you and do "real" jobs. Short bursts of play (two to ten minutes) are normal.

| Activity | How | Talk tip |
|---|---|---|
| Pots-and-lids cupboard | Give your toddler one low cupboard of safe pots, lids and plastic bowls. | "In! Out! In again!" Repeat what they say and add one word. |
| Box tunnel | Open both ends of a large cardboard box. | "Where did you go? There you are!" |
| Sock basket match | Big socks, one basket. Find the pairs together. | "Blue sock. Where's the other blue sock?" |
| Wash the toys | A shallow tub of water, a cloth and a few chunky toys. | "Wash, wash, wash. All clean!" |
| Follow-the-leader walk | March, stomp, tiptoe, stop. | Take turns being the leader and name each move. |

**Safety note:** stay within arm's reach during water play and empty the tub straight away. Check every object against the toilet-paper-tube test.

## Preschoolers: 3 to 5 years

Pretend play takes off. Preschoolers can follow two- and three-step games and love to be in charge.

| Activity | How | Talk tip |
|---|---|---|
| Pretend café | A table, a notepad, toy food or real (safe) snacks. | Let your child take your order and ask questions: "What do you recommend?" |
| Tape road | An adult sticks painter's tape roads on the floor for toy cars. | "Where is your car driving? What's at the end of the road?" |
| Story cards | Draw simple pictures on cards (sun, dog, house) and turn one over at a time. | Take turns adding one sentence to a silly story. |
| Nature hunt | Find something rough, smooth, green, tiny, bigger than your hand. | "How does it feel? What else feels like that?" |
| Freeze dance | You sing or play music; everyone freezes when it stops. | Name the freeze pose: "You're a statue of a… flamingo!" |

**Safety note:** an adult puts down and picks up tape pieces. If under-3s are around, keep small craft pieces out of reach.

## Ages 5 to 8

School-age kids want challenge, rules and a chance to win. They also still love it when a grown-up joins in.

| Activity | How | Talk tip |
|---|---|---|
| Card games | Go Fish, Crazy Eights, War, with a standard deck. | Chat about strategy: "Why did you keep that card?" |
| Build-a-fort | Blankets, chairs and clothespins. | Plan together: "Where's the door? What's the fort's name?" |
| Kitchen helper | Measure, pour, stir and read a simple recipe aloud. | Ask them to read the next step to you. |
| Paper airplane contest | Fold three designs and test which goes farthest. | "What do you think made that one fly farther?" |
| Comic strip | Fold paper into four boxes and draw a story. | "What happens next? What does the character say?" |

**Safety note:** an adult handles the stove, oven and sharp knives.

## Ages 9 to 12

Older kids want independence and real responsibility. Offer choices, not orders.

| Activity | How | Talk tip |
|---|---|---|
| Plan a family meal | They choose the menu, write the list and cook with help. | Ask about their choices, not just the result. |
| Board-game night | Let them pick the game and teach the rules. | Being the teacher is good practice at explaining. |
| Build something | Cardboard marble run, birdhouse kit, a model. | "What's the trickiest part? What would you change?" |
| Start a collection or a notebook | Rocks, leaves, sketches, a reading log, a weather journal. | Let them show you and tell you what's new. |
| Neighborhood map | Draw a map of the block or a favorite park from memory, then check it on a walk. | "What did you remember that I forgot?" |

**Safety note:** agree on boundaries and check-in times for any outdoor independence.

## Why play like this is worth a few minutes

For the youngest children, the World Health Organization's 2019 guideline encourages plenty of active play (at least 180 minutes a day of varied physical activity for ages 1 to 4) and reading and storytelling with a caregiver when children are sitting still. The American Academy of Pediatrics' 2016 statement "Media and Young Minds" stresses unstructured play and talking together for young children. You don't need to count minutes. Just notice how many of the ideas above involve **moving, talking and a grown-up nearby**. That's the common thread.

## A simple way to start

- [ ] Choose **one** activity for your child's age.
- [ ] Set it up before the moment screens usually appear (before dinner prep, after school).
- [ ] Join in for the first two minutes, then step back if your child is happily playing.
- [ ] Try the same activity three days in a row. Children love repeating things, and it gets easier each time.

## What this does not mean

Screen-free play isn't about being a perfect parent or banning screens forever. Screens have a place in family life. This list is simply a stock of easy, low-prep ideas for the moments when you'd like an alternative.
