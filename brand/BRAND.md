# Play Before Pixels — Brand Kit (controls every product)

## Who we are
A parent-education brand for families of babies and toddlers (0–5) **and** school-age children (5–12), plus the parent groups, PTAs and teacher/early-childhood groups who serve them. The second audience is the same crowd that reads books like *The Digital Delusion*: parents and educators who want less screen-based learning and more paper, talk and play at home and in classrooms. Classroom material is research-based and general — it helps people ask good questions; it never names or criticizes a school, district or product. Message: the first years are built on talk, touch and play; fewer screens, more back-and-forth. Tone: warm, plain, practical, zero shame, zero preaching. Never scary.

## HARD RULES (a product that breaks one is rejected)
1. **No health or medical claims.** Nothing treats, cures, prevents or reverses autism, delay or any condition. Never "therapy", "speech therapy", "clinically proven", "cure", "reverse", "heal". Coaching and books are parent education. The founder is **not** a speech-language pathologist; never imply SLP authorship or clinical credentials.
2. **Never name, rate or criticize** any school, district, company, app, device brand or EdTech product. No Montgomery County or any local angle. No logos of real products. A generic tablet/phone may appear only as a neutral object.
3. **"Virtual autism"** may appear only in research-hub material, always framed as "a term some clinicians use", "not a medical diagnosis", and with associations never stated as causation. It never appears on merchandise or in children's books.
4. **Child safety in every activity:** adult supervision; for children under 3 no object small enough to fit through a toilet-paper tube (CPSC small-parts rule of thumb, ~1.25 in / 3.17 cm diameter); no balloons for under-8s; no cords/strings long enough to wrap a neck; water play always supervised; no food items that are choking hazards for toddlers (whole grapes, nuts, popcorn, hard candy).
5. **Only these research citations may be used** (all verified): Madigan S et al., JAMA Pediatrics 2019;173(3):244–250 · Heffler KF et al., JAMA Pediatrics 2020;174(7):690–696 · Kushima M et al., JAMA Pediatrics 2022;176(4):384–391 (association in boys, not girls) · Takahashi I et al., JAMA Pediatrics 2023;177(10):1039–1046 (7,097 children; dose-response link to communication and problem-solving delay at 2 and 4) · Brushe ME et al., JAMA Pediatrics 2024;178(4):369–375 (at 36 months: 1,139 fewer adult words, 843 fewer child vocalizations, 194 fewer conversational turns per day) · Harlé B, Trends in Neuroscience and Education 2019;17:100119 (hypothesis paper proposing "virtual autism") · WHO 2019 under-5 guidelines (no screen time under 1; ≤1 hr/day ages 2–4; 180 min active play ages 1–4) · AAP "Media and Young Minds," Pediatrics 2016;138(5) · UNESCO GEM Report 2023 "Technology in education" · Delgado P et al., Educational Research Review 2018;25:23–38 (54 studies, 171,000+ readers, paper advantage). Anything else must be marked "[VERIFY]" and not presented as fact.
6. Talk strategies may be described in plain words (pause and wait, say what you see, repeat and add one word, offer a choice, follow their lead, sing and gesture) — **do not use trademarked program names** (e.g., Hanen, "It Takes Two to Talk", "More Than Words").
7. Do not copy any other brand's titles, characters, page designs or trade dress. Be inspired by the quality bar only.

## Illustration style (inline SVG, drawn by you)
Flat vector, solid fills, no gradients, no drop shadows, no outlines (or at most a 0 stroke). Characters are built from simple geometry: round heads, dot eyes, a short curved smile, rounded-rectangle bodies, stubby limbs. Objects are big, centered and unmistakable at a glance (a toddler must recognize "ball" instantly). One focal object per board-book page. Generous negative space. Use the brand palette only. Consistency: define reusable SVG `<symbol>`s (child, grown-up, ball, blocks, cup, shoe, dog, cat, duck, sun, moon, book, tablet-sleeping) and reuse them so characters look the same on every page.

## Print conventions
- Include bleed: 0.125 in (3.175 mm) on every trimmed edge; keep text and faces inside a 0.375 in safe zone from trim.
- Page size via CSS `@page { size: W H; margin: 0 }` and `.page { width: W; height: H; page-break-after: always; overflow: hidden; position: relative }`.
- Board books: 6 × 6 in trim (6.25 × 6.25 in with bleed). Picture books: 8.5 × 8.5 in trim (8.75 × 8.75 in with bleed), 32 pages. Paperback guide: 8 × 10 in trim (8.25 × 10.25 in with bleed). Printables: US Letter 8.5 × 11 in (no bleed, 0.5 in margins) AND an A4 version is a bonus for international buyers. Cards: 2.5 × 3.5 in poker size with 0.125 in bleed.
- Mark in every listing that the final file must be checked against the chosen printer's current template (KDP, IngramSpark, Lulu, etc.) before upload.
- ISBN/barcode: leave a clearly labelled white box on back covers (2 × 1.2 in) — "ISBN / barcode".
