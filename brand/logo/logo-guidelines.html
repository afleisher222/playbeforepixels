The idea

The Maker’s Seal

Our logo is a maker’s seal, like the stamp pressed into the bottom of a good wooden toy, with our name around the edge and a spinning top in the middle.

A top is one of the oldest toys there is and needs nothing but a child’s hand, which is the whole idea: play comes first.

The name is part of the mark, so it never has to be decoded, and the only letters anywhere are the words of the name. The top is a toy, not an initial.

Room to breathe

Clear space

Keep the shaded zone clear of text, edges, other logos and busy photo detail. With a seal: one fifth of the seal’s diameter all round (in the lockups, one fifth of the small seal). Wordmark alone: half the height of its capital P. The files are cropped to the artwork, so add this space yourself.

Seal with words: 80 px on screen, 16 mm in print. At 16 mm the capitals are 1.54 mm tall, safely above the 1.2 mm print limit. Anything smaller uses the small seal.

Embroidery: the seal with words from 60 mm (5.8 mm capitals); ask the embroiderer to check the letter gaps. The small seal from 25 mm. One-colour stitching uses the black or white files, which keep the band as a stencil stripe.

Small cut of the wordmark: Bricolage’s optical size 14, tracking +30, word space 226, so the heavy letters do not fill in on spines and labels.

Rebuilding prints the smallest gaps at every one of these sizes, so an edit that closes a gap shows at once.

Colour

The disc is only ever ink or paper, or black or white in one colour: never sky, tomato, sun yellow or gold. The one-colour files are single even-odd paths: every letter, ball and the top are true holes, and the band is a stencil stripe that stops short of each edge, so the top never falls apart when it is cut from vinyl or stitched.

Placement

Books and merch

  • Never print the seal in gold, foil or sun yellow, and never add laurels, ribbons, stars or a starburst edge. It is a maker’s stamp, not a medal.
  • Spine or back cover only on books; on printables, the footer; on merch, wherever the product template puts it.
  • Never set the seal and the wordmark side by side: the name would appear twice. The lockups pair the small seal with the name.
Play Before Pixels · Logo guidelines09

Keep it the same everywhere

Do and don’t

Why no initials. PB reads as PBS, as “peanut butter” (a nut-allergy word in classrooms) and as Pb, the symbol for lead. BP is an oil company. PP is ruled out by BRAND.md. pbp is built from b and p, the mirror-image pair early readers mix up.

Before the logo is adopted

  1. The founder makes her own dated edits in src/build.py (the band colour, the lean and at least one choice of her own), rebuilds, and logs each one.
  2. Blind check: show blind-test/test-small.png, as a real browser tab, to 5–10 parents who have never seen the brand and ask “what is this?”. Adopt only if most say “spinning top” or “toy” and nobody names a letter.

Before filing or printing in bulk

  1. Reverse-image search of mark.svg, mark-small.svg and favicon.svg.
  2. USPTO design-code search (toys and tops; circles with lettering, 26.01) in classes 9, 16, 25, 28, 35 and 41, then an attorney’s knockout search.
  3. File in AlphaPlay LLC’s name with an attorney as correspondent: the seal with its words, plus the name as a standard-character wordmark. Never the bare top.
