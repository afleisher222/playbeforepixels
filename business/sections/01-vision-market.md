## 1.1 Vision

Families of young children, and the groups that serve them, should be able to find a well-made, ready-to-use tool for talk, touch and play at the moment they need one. That might be 5 p.m. on a weekday, a long wait at the doctor's office, a road trip, a baby shower or a parent night. The tool should be on their phone within a minute, on their doorstep within a week, and in time on the shelf of the stores where they already shop, Target included.

Getting there takes three stages, and the order matters.

1. **Online proof (months 0–12).** A focused line of printables and print-on-demand books sells on our own site, Etsy and Amazon (KDP). It builds a sales history, honest reviews and an owned email list.
2. **Series and physical formats (year 2 onward, gated).** The winners become series, bundles, paperbacks and print-on-demand card decks. A true offset-printed board book comes only when its gate is met (section 3.10 rule 4): the print-on-demand paperback proves demand, and retained earnings, not a customer pre-sale, pay for the run. A fulfillment warehouse (3PL) or Amazon (FBA) stores and ships it, never the founder.
3. **Retail (conditional on volume, not on a date).** Passive availability comes first. The IngramSpark paperbacks can be ordered by bookstores and libraries, and may appear on target.com and walmart.com through wholesaler feeds without a pitch [VERIFY]. Walmart Marketplace (by application) and wholesale follow once there are three physical products whose landed cost meets each channel's cost rule. Target Plus is invitation-only [VERIFY], so it is an outcome, not a task. A pitch to chain buyers, made through a distributor or rep, comes only with the unit volumes in section 4.9 (`marketing/AMAZON-AND-RETAIL-ROADMAP.md` §B).

The roadmap calls these "milestones, not promises", and this plan treats them the same way. Getting into Target is the long-range goal that decides what we build first. It is not a date. On the plan's own Expected numbers, a store shelf is not reachable within the 36-month horizon, and realistically not before 2030 (section 4.9).

## 1.2 Mission

**Make the unplugged hours the best part of the day.** We do it with age-banded play-and-talk products that:

- are warm and practical, and never shame a family;
- are honest about the research (studies show links, not proof of cause);
- are safe by design, meeting brand rule 4 on every activity;
- sell and deliver themselves, with no founder on camera, no live services and no inventory in the founder's hands.

The message stays the same across the whole line: *the first years are built on talk, touch and play; fewer screens, more back-and-forth* (`brand/BRAND.md`). The campaign line is *"The tablet's napping. The world is open."* (`marketing/CAMPAIGN-BIBLE.md` §1).

This business is designed to run on about 5 founder hours a week (`marketing/MARKETING-PLAYBOOK.md`), with scheduled Claude Code routines doing the research, building, listing, content and bookkeeping (`ops/ROUTINE.md`). Five binding rules shape every expansion choice in this plan:

## 1.3 The problem

**Parents are told to cut screen time, but get little help with what to do instead at the moment it matters.**

- **Official guidance sets limits without giving the alternative.** The WHO guideline advises no screen time under age 1, no more than 1 hour a day for ages 2–4, and 180 minutes of varied physical activity a day for ages 1–4 (WHO 2019). The AAP's "Media and Young Minds" (Pediatrics 2016) sets out similar principles. Neither gives a parent a ready answer for a hungry toddler at 5 p.m.
- **The research is about lost back-and-forth, and it shows associations.** One study estimated that, at 36 months, higher screen time was linked with about 1,139 fewer adult words, 843 fewer child vocalizations and 194 fewer back-and-forth turns a day. It shows a link, not proof of cause (Brushe et al., JAMA Pediatrics 2024). A study of 7,097 children found a dose-response link between screen time and communication and problem-solving delay at ages 2 and 4 (Takahashi et al., JAMA Pediatrics 2023). This is the gap our products fill: more talk, turn by turn, during ordinary moments.
- **What exists is either free and plain, or paid and built on something we will not use.** Free guidance and free blog lists are everywhere, but they are not age-banded, designed or ready to print. Paid products usually lean on clinical credentials, therapy or autism keywords, a founder's face on video, or physical inventory (`marketing/DEMAND-CHECK.md`).
- **Educators and groups want low-prep, paper-based material they can run themselves.** Teachers want "low-prep activities ready for tomorrow" and something they can use on Monday. Groups want "a program they can run without an outside expert" (`marketing/MARKETING-PLAYBOOK.md`, Segments 1, 2 and 4). A school or PTA also needs a clean paper trail: quote, invoice, W-9 and PO acceptance.
- **Screen-free is usually sold through guilt or fear.** Much of the category's copy uses addiction, "brain rot" or scare language. Our voice guide bans all of it (`marketing/CAMPAIGN-BIBLE.md` §1). Many parents who used a tablet at dinner last night would still welcome an offer that doesn't judge them.

**What we will never say:** that any product treats, prevents or relates to autism, delay or any condition. Any worry about a child's development is referred to the pediatrician and free early intervention (brand rules 1 and 3).

## 1.7 Positioning

**Positioning statement.** For parents of children from birth to 12, and later the groups who serve them, who want fewer screens without guilt, Play Before Pixels is the play-and-talk line organized by age. Every item carries a grown-up talk line, a safety note and honest research wording, and leads to the next stage. It is instant as a download, printed on demand as a book, and designed as a collectable series. It sells without clinical claims, fear, or a personality on camera.

**Proof points we can already show, or will show by launch:**

- One content engine, the plays library, feeds the book, the card products and the printables. Every item uses the same six plain-words talk moves: pause and wait, say what you see, repeat and add one word, offer a choice, follow their lead, sing and gesture.
- Age bands on everything: 0–1, 1–2, 2–3 and 3–5 for toddlers; 1–3, 3–5, 5–8 and 8–12 for the bored cards.
- The files buyers expect: editable, US Letter and A4, Monday and Sunday starts, several colorways (DEMAND-CHECK pricing rule 6).
- A number in every title ("200+ Routine Cards", "150 Screen-Free Play Cards").
- "Every product leads to the next": a QR bonus, next-age recommendations and series strips (`brand/BRAND.md`).

## 1.9 The research hub: education, not sales

**What it is.** A free library of plain-language summaries of peer-reviewed studies and official guidelines on young children, screens, talk and reading. It lives at **playbeforepixels.com/research/** under a non-medical name ("Research Library" in `seo/SEO-PLAN.md`). The earlier working name, "The Virtual Autism Project", is retired: `legal/DECISION-MEMO.json` requires the rename because of health-claim risk, and virtualautism.org is already in use by someone else. `marketing/CAMPAIGN-BIBLE.md` §4 still uses the old name and should be updated.

**Its job:**

1. **Give worried parents calm, accurate information, and remove blame.** Every piece that discusses the term "virtual autism" uses the approved framing sentence: *a term some clinicians use; not a medical diagnosis; studies show links, not proof of cause; autism is not a parent's fault.* Every such piece closes by sending the reader to their pediatrician and free early intervention (`marketing/CAMPAIGN-BIBLE.md` §4; brand rule 3).
2. **Be the single home for research wording.** Only the approved citations in brand rule 5 are used, always stated as associations. Anything else is marked [VERIFY] and not presented as fact.
3. **Earn credibility that sales pages cannot.** A calm, sourced hub with a published editorial policy and a dated "What We Changed" log gives a librarian, a PTA leader or a retail buyer a way to judge our standards without a call.

**What it is not.** It is not a sales channel.

- No product, price or mascot appears on or beside any autism-term piece.
- Hub pages are kept separate from ad landing pages. They are never boosted, never used as an ad hook, never tagged #autism, and never aimed at people searching for autism.
- They may invite readers to the free play printable and the email list, framed as play and family time. They never present it as help for autism (brand "Autism searches" rule).
- Every piece goes through the weekly approval queue. Each format gets a paid sensitivity read before it is first published.

**Contents:** Study Snapshots cards and captions-only one-minute episodes (one a week, steady all year, never timed to awareness observances); the Solid, Shaky, or Not Shown sorting game; the free What We Know, What We Don't brief and term page; the Wondering Page for pediatrician visits (no symptom checklist, no email gate, no product); a plain-words glossary; and a straight-answers FAQ.

**How we measure it:** not by revenue. We track corrections made and logged; links earned from library and nonprofit "resources for families" pages; sign-ups to the free printable from hub pages, which is play-framed and never product-framed; and zero compliance-gate failures.
