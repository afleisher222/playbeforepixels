## 1. Keep the LLC shield strong

**What the shield does and does not do.** Under Md. Code, Corps. & Ass'ns §4A-301, a member is not personally liable for LLC obligations "solely by reason of being a member." In *Allen v. Dackman*, 413 Md. 132 (2010), Maryland's high court held that a member who personally "commits, inspires or participates in" a tort can be personally liable. Arielle personally writes the books, runs the coaching and teaches the workshops, so her own negligence or defamation can reach her directly. The LLC is the first layer of protection. Insurance (section 2), contracts (section 3) and careful content (section 4) are the other layers.
Sources: https://law.justia.com/codes/maryland/corporations-and-associations/title-4a/subtitle-3/section-4a-301/ · https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/md-court-of-appeals/1522965.html

**Veil piercing.** Maryland pierces the veil only "to prevent fraud or to enforce a paramount equity" (*Hildreth v. Tidewater Equipment Co.*, 378 Md. 724 (2003)). Commingling, missing records and undercapitalization feed the paramount-equity argument. Because she sells worldwide, she could be sued in states with looser tests (not verified state by state).
Source: https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2399227/hildreth-v-tidewater-equipment-co/

**Separateness habits (do now):**
- [ ] Open a business checking account and a business card in AlphaPlay LLC's name under its EIN. Send every marketplace, KDP, IngramSpark and affiliate payout **only** to that account. Cost: $0–$15/mo (unverified).
- [ ] Pay no personal expenses from the business account. Pay herself by recorded owner draws. If she pays a business cost personally, record it as a capital contribution or a reimbursement.
- [ ] Keep enough money or insurance in the LLC to cover foreseeable claims. Undercapitalization is a piercing factor.
- [ ] Use bookkeeping software linked only to the business account. Cost: about $0–$30/mo (unverified).
- [ ] Keep a records folder with the articles, EIN letter, operating agreement, annual reports, insurance policies, contracts and a simple annual member consent.

**Sign only as the LLC (now, $0).** Use this signature block on every contract, invoice and agreement: *"AlphaPlay LLC d/b/a Play Before Pixels, By: ____ Arielle Fleisher, Member."* Put the LLC's legal name in every contract preamble. Open KDP, IngramSpark, Amazon, Etsy, TpT, TikTok Shop, Meta, Faire, Shopify, payment and ad accounts in the LLC's name with the EIN, never her SSN. Where a platform offers only an "individual" account, ask the attorney whether to migrate it. Decline personal guarantees. §4A-301 does not protect a guarantee because it is her own promise. If a lender or landlord insists, negotiate a dollar cap and a sunset date.

**Trade name (now).** File the SDAT Trade Name Application listing AlphaPlay LLC as owner of "Play Before Pixels". Cost: **$25** ($50 expedited). The registration lasts 5 years from acceptance. Renew online in the last 6 months of the term or SDAT forfeits the name. The renewal fee was not found (unverified); SDAT trade-name help is 410-767-1801 or sdat.tradenamehelp@maryland.gov. A trade name is **not** a trademark and gives no rights against copycats.
Sources: https://dat.maryland.gov/businesses/documents/fees.pdf · https://dat.maryland.gov/SiteAssets/Pages/sdatforms/Trade%20Name%20Application%20%28Registration%29%20and%20Instructions_0826-A.pdf

**Good standing (every year).** File SDAT Form 1 (Annual Report and Business Personal Property Return) through Maryland Business Express by **April 15**. Cost: **$300/yr**. A late return is penalized at 1/10 of 1% of the county assessment, plus 2% interest on that penalty for each 30 days late. Set reminders for **March 1** and **April 1**. Source: https://dat.maryland.gov/SiteAssets/Pages/sdatforms/2026%20Form%201%20Instructions%20%20FINAL.pdf

**Resident agent and principal office (now).** Every Maryland LLC must keep a Maryland principal office and a resident agent (§4A-210). SDAT forms say the agent address must be a physical Maryland street address. P.O. boxes, mail-rental facilities, virtual addresses, UPS Stores and similar addresses are not accepted, and the same exclusion applies to the principal office. **The Rockville Pike CMRA box cannot be the principal office or the agent address.**
- Engage a Maryland commercial resident agent. Cost: about $50–$300/yr (unverified).
- Ask the attorney or agent whether the agent's office can also serve as the principal office. If not, use a real staffed office.
- If her home address is on file now, file the Resolution to Change Principal Office/Resident Agent. Cost: **$25** ($50 expedited).
- Ask the attorney whether older filings that show her home address can be redacted.

Sources: https://law.justia.com/codes/maryland/corporations-and-associations/title-4a/subtitle-2/section-4a-210/ · https://dat.maryland.gov/SDAT%20Forms/RA-address-change.pdf

**Operating agreement and IP assignment (now).** Maryland does not require a written operating agreement (§4A-402), but having one helps against veil-piercing arguments and settles who owns the IP. Have the attorney draft or refresh a single-member agreement covering:

- capital contributions
- a successor or designee if she is incapacitated or dies (coordinate with her estate plan)
- a separate **written IP assignment** from Arielle to AlphaPlay LLC (see section 6)

Cost: about $500–$2,000 (unverified).
Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/maryland/corporations-and-associations/title-4a/subtitle-4/section-4a-402/

**BOI: nothing to file.** FinCEN's final rule of Aug 11, 2026, effective Aug 14, 2026, permanently removes beneficial-ownership reporting for U.S.-formed entities. Ignore paid "BOI compliance" mailers. Cost: $0.

## 4. Content and claims

**Health and developmental claims (now, $0).** The FTC's Health Products Compliance Guidance (Dec. 2022) applies to all health-related claims and requires competent and reliable scientific evidence. A disclaimer does not cure an unsupported claim.
- Keep a **claims file**: each claim, its supporting studies and the date checked.
- Replace outcome claims ("prevents speech delay", "boosts IQ") with hedged, cited statements whose strength matches the study design.
- Apply the same rules to merch slogans and TikTok and Meta ads.
- Optional attorney ad review: about $300–$1,000 (unverified).

Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance

**Affiliate and endorsement disclosures (now, $0).**
- Put "Paid link" or "I earn a commission if you buy through this link" right next to each link. In video, say it and show it on screen.
- A footer note alone, or only the platform's built-in tag, is not enough.
- Use real testimonials only, with written permission. Disclose free products.

Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking

**Defamation-avoidance house rules (now, $0).** A Maryland defamation plaintiff must show a false, defamatory statement to a third person, made with legal fault, that caused harm (*Piscatelli v. Van Smith*, 424 Md. 294 (2012)).
1. Never name or identify specific schools, districts, teachers, students, employers or companies in a negative light. Write in generalities.
2. Criticize products or practices only with verifiable, linked facts, framed as opinion.
3. Use no anecdotes traceable to real children or families.
4. Keep a pre-publication checklist and a corrections policy.
5. Send anything touching schools or education policy to the attorney first.

**Anti-SLAPP status is unsettled (unverified).** Current Md. Code, Cts. & Jud. Proc. §5-807 is narrow: it requires a bad-faith suit and has no fee-shifting. The 2026 bills SB 251 / HB 650 (Uniform Public Expression Protection Act) carry an **Oct 1, 2026** effective date and would replace it with an expedited motion to dismiss and fee provisions. Whether they were enacted was **not** confirmed. Do not rely on either version until the attorney confirms.
Sources: https://law.justia.com/cases/maryland/court-of-appeals/2012/18-11.html · https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcj&section=5-807 · https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/fnotes/bil_0001/sb0251.pdf

**Research hub fair use (now, $0).**
- Summarize in her own words. Quote only the short passages needed for commentary.
- Link to the publisher or DOI instead of hosting PDFs.
- Do not reproduce figures without permission or an open license. Attribute CC-BY material exactly.
- Give the full citation and the date reviewed. Note study limitations.

Source: https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/

**CPSIA for children's products (before the first sale).**
- The CPSC's "ordinary book" testing exemption does **not** cover books with inherent play value, books designed or intended for children **3 or younger**, or toys packaged with a book. **Board books for ages 0–3 likely need third-party testing and a Children's Product Certificate (CPC).**
- For each SKU, decide the intended age. Any card deck or merch primarily intended for children 12 or under is a children's product.
- Get the POD printer's reports from a CPSC-accepted lab and issue a CPC ($0 to prepare). Lab testing often costs a few hundred dollars per product (unverified).
- Where a book relies on the ordinary-book exemption, CPC section 6 must cite PL 112-28 and state that the book meets the ordinary-book definition.
- Put tracking information (printer, date, batch) on the copyright page or packaging.
- Position the parent card deck and adult merch as adult products only where that is true.

Sources: https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/Childrens-Books · https://www.cpsc.gov/Testing-Certification/Childrens-Product-Certificate-CPC · https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/tracking-label

### 7b. Takedown playbook: the escalation ladder
1. **Screenshot and log** the page (date, URL, seller name).
2. **Check the claim.** Did they copy protected expression (text, art, layout), or only an idea, activity type or common phrase? Consider fair use. A knowing material misrepresentation in a DMCA notice makes the sender liable for damages and fees (§512(f)).
3. **File a platform report** (see the table below and `marketplace-ip-report-checklist.md`) or a **DMCA notice** (`dmca-takedown-notice.md`) as AlphaPlay LLC once the assignment is signed.
4. **Ask search engines to delist** pirate sites (Google Legal Help troubleshooter; Microsoft's form for Bing). Send a DMCA notice to the site's host at the same time.
5. **If a counter-notice arrives, call the attorney the same day.** Under §512(g) the host restores the material in 10–14 business days unless the owner reports that it has filed suit, which requires a registration or a refusal first.
6. **Demand letter** through the attorney.
7. **Copyright Claims Board** for claims under $30,000. Filing costs $40, plus $60 if the case proceeds ($100 total). Respondents have 60 days to opt out. A pending application can be expedited for **$50** (about 10 business days) instead of the $800 special handling fee. Damage caps: $15,000 per work if timely registered, $7,500 per work (maximum $15,000 per proceeding) if not, and $30,000 per proceeding overall.
8. **Federal court**, with the attorney.

## ⚠️ Added September 28, 2026 — AI-assisted products and copyright (read this before registering anything)

**The rule.** U.S. copyright protects only human authorship. The U.S. Copyright Office's *Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability* (January 2025) concludes that purely AI-generated material is not protected, and that prompting alone is not enough control to make the user an author. Human contributions **are** protected where they are perceptible in the work, including creative selection, coordination or arrangement of material and creative modifications of AI outputs. When registering, the applicant must disclose and exclude ("disclaim") AI-generated material that is more than de minimis. On March 2, 2026 the U.S. Supreme Court denied certiorari in *Thaler v. Perlmutter*, leaving in place the D.C. Circuit's holding that the Copyright Act requires a human author.

**What this means for Play Before Pixels.** The books, illustrations, printables and designs in this repository were drafted with Claude Code (AI). As delivered, much of that material may not be copyrightable, so a copycat who reproduces it could argue there is nothing to infringe. Filing a registration that claims AI-generated material as human-authored can invalidate the registration.

**How to protect the products anyway (in order of strength):**
1. **Trademark is unaffected.** PLAY BEFORE PIXELS, book-series names and the logo are protected by trademark use and registration regardless of how the products were made. This is the strongest anti-copycat tool and does not depend on authorship. File it first.
2. **Make Arielle the human author of the parts that matter most.** She rewrites the text of each book in her own words, chooses and arranges the pages, changes compositions, colors and characters, and adds her own material. Keep the before/after files in this repository (git history is dated proof) and log her decisions in `creation-records-log.md`. Her human contributions, selection and arrangement are registrable; the AI-generated remainder is disclaimed.
3. **Commission human illustrators for flagship titles** under the work-for-hire and IP-assignment agreement in this folder. Human-drawn art assigned to AlphaPlay LLC is fully protected. Use the AI drafts as the art brief.
4. **Contract terms still bind buyers.** The digital-product license (personal / single classroom / site license; no resale or redistribution) is enforceable against purchasers as a contract whether or not the content is copyrightable.
5. **Register only what is human-authored, with AI material disclaimed**, following the Copyright Office's registration guidance for works containing AI-generated material. Let the IP attorney prepare the first registrations.
6. **Speed, brand and email list** remain the practical moat: a large, consistent, trademarked line sold everywhere first.

Sources: U.S. Copyright Office, Copyright and Artificial Intelligence Part 2 (Jan. 2025), https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html · Supreme Court denial of certiorari in Thaler v. Perlmutter (Mar. 2, 2026), e.g. https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/ip-updates/supreme-court-declines-to-hear-thaler-v-perlmutter-leaving-human-authorship-requirement-intact.html
