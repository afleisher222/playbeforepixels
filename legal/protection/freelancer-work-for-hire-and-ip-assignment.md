# Freelance Services, Work-Made-for-Hire and IP Assignment Agreement

*For illustrators, designers, editors, expert reviewers and translators working for AlphaPlay LLC d/b/a Play Before Pixels.*

> **Preparer notes (delete before sending)**
> - Have this signed **before** any work starts. Under 17 U.S.C. §204(a), a copyright transfer is valid only in a writing signed by the owner. A commissioned work is "made for hire" only if (1) it falls within one of the nine categories in 17 U.S.C. §101 and (2) both parties sign a written instrument saying so (U.S. Copyright Office Circular 30, https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ30.pdf). The nine categories are: a contribution to a collective work; part of a motion picture or other audiovisual work; a translation; a supplementary work; a compilation; an instructional text; a test; answer material for a test; and an atlas. The attorney decides whether a given job fits a category. The backup assignment in section 3 covers everything else.
> - **California creators:** under Cal. Labor Code §3351.5(c) and Unemp. Ins. Code §686, a work-for-hire clause can make a California individual a statutory employee (https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-3351-5/). For a California creator, choose **Option B** in section 2 (assignment only).
> - Section 7 governs confidential information, so it must keep the Defend Trade Secrets Act notice (18 U.S.C. §1833(b), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833). Without that notice, AlphaPlay cannot recover exemplary damages or attorney's fees under the DTSA against this person.
> - Keep the signed PDF in the IP folder of the company minute book, and log it in `creation-records-log.md`.
