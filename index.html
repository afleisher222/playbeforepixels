What the pediatric guidelines say

How much screen time, by age

Two major bodies have published guidance for the youngest children. They agree on the direction: less is better, and none is best for babies.

Under12 moNo screen time
WHO, 2019. Video chat with family is the usual exception.
Under18–24 moAvoid digital media except video chat
American Academy of Pediatrics, 2016.
Ages2–4 yrNo more than 1 hour a day, less is better
WHO, 2019. AAP: high-quality programming, watched together.
Instead3+ hrOf active play spread through the day
WHO, 2019, for 1- to 4-year-olds.

Sources: World Health Organization, Guidelines on physical activity, sedentary behaviour and sleep for children under 5 years of age (2019). AAP Council on Communications and Media, "Media and Young Minds," Pediatrics 138(5), 2016. Bars show daily screen allowance on a four-hour scale.

What the research does not say

These studies show associations, not causes, and they describe groups, not any one child. Nothing here is about blame. If you are worried about your child's development, talk to your pediatrician. Every U.S. state runs a free early intervention program for children under 3, and you can ask for an evaluation yourself.

2019

More screen time, lower developmental scores

In about 2,400 Canadian children, higher screen time at 24 and 36 months was linked to poorer developmental screening results a year later.

Madigan S et al. JAMA Pediatrics 173(3):244–250.
2023

Delays in communication

In 7,097 Japanese children, screen time at age 1 showed a dose-response link to communication and problem-solving delays at ages 2 and 4.

Takahashi I et al. JAMA Pediatrics 177(10):1039–1046.
2024

Screens crowd out talk

Recorders worn by Australian toddlers found that more screen time meant fewer words from adults and fewer back-and-forth exchanges. At 36 months, the daily gap looked like this:

1,139fewer adult words
843fewer child vocalizations
194fewer conversational turns
Brushe ME et al. JAMA Pediatrics 178(4):369–375.

Screens in the classroom

The same question, at school

Many children now spend part of the school day on devices. The research on learning asks the same thing parents ask at home: does the screen help, or does it get in the way?

UNESCO · 2023

Use tech only where it helps learning

The UN's global education report found little strong evidence for most classroom technology. It recommends using it only when it improves learning, and notes that about one in four countries has banned smartphones in school.

UNESCO, Global Education Monitoring Report 2023: Technology in education.

Meta-analysis · 2018

Paper beats screens for reading

A review of 54 studies with more than 171,000 readers found better comprehension when reading on paper than on screens, especially for informational text and timed reading.

Delgado P et al. Educational Research Review 25:23–38.

OECD PISA · 2022

Distraction costs points

In the OECD's international assessment of 15-year-olds, students who said they were distracted by digital devices in math class scored lower in math than students who were not.

OECD, PISA 2022 Results, Volume II.
