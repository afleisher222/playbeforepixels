For grown-ups

5 lap games for tiny ones

No batteries, no screens—just you, a lap and a few silly minutes. Keep games short, stop while it’s still fun, and let your little one ask for “more” in any way they can.

1

Pony Ride

Once your baby holds their head steady

Sit your little one on your knees, facing you, with a firm hold under the arms. Chant “Trot, trot, trot…” with small bounces. On “Whoa!”, stop—and wait for a look, a wiggle or a word that says “more.”

Safe play: Small, gentle bounces only. Never shake, toss or drop a baby.

2

Row the Boat

Babies who can sit, toddlers, big kids

Face to face, hold hands or forearms and rock slowly forward and back while you sing a rowing song. Stop the boat mid-song. Wait for them to rock you to start again.

Safe play: Rock slowly. Never pull, swing or lift a child by the hands or arms.

3

Peekaboo Lap

From the early months

Cover your own face with your hands or a light cloth. “Where did I go?” Pause… “Peekaboo!” Soon they will pull the cloth away themselves.

Safe play: Cover your face, not theirs. Put the cloth away afterward—never leave one with a sleeping baby.

4

Toe Count

All ages

Hold a foot and count the toes: “One, two, three, four…” Stop before “FIVE!” and wait for a squeal, a wiggle or a word. Then do fingers.

Safe play: Gentle touches. If they pull away, that’s a “no”—switch games.

5

Lap Drum

Toddlers and up

Pat a beat on your knees—slow, fast, soft, loud. They copy you. Then swap: they lead and you copy them. A sturdy board book makes a great drum.

Safe play: Hands and board books only. For under-3s, nothing small enough to fit through a toilet-paper tube.

Lap-time safety

  • An adult stays close and hands-on for every game.
  • For children under 3: no objects small enough to fit through a toilet-paper tube (about 1.25 in / 3.2 cm), no balloons, and no cords or strings long enough to wrap a neck.
  • In a car, children ride in a properly installed car seat—never on a lap. On buses and trains, follow local rules and hold on securely.
  • Sleepy snuggles are lovely. When a baby falls asleep, move them to their own safe sleep space, on their back.
  • Stop any game when your child looks away, fusses or says “no.”
A note for grown-ups

A lap is a small place with a big job.

When your child is close enough to feel your voice, reading turns into a conversation. You say something, you pause, and you wait for their turn—a look, a point, a sound, a word. That back-and-forth is the heart of this book, and every spread has a Lap talk idea to help it along.

There is no right way to read it. Skip pages. Make up voices. Read the same page ten times. The goal isn’t finishing the book—it’s the talking along the way. And your phone can rest face-down for a few minutes. Your little one will notice.
