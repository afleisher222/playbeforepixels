On Saturday morning,Ada zoomed downstairs.Her slippers were onthe wrong feet.She didn’t care one bit.
But the tablet was wearing a nightcap.Its eyes were shut tight.And it was going…zzz-bip… zzz-bip…
“Shhh,” whispered Papa.“The tablet is sleeping.Today, it gets a day off.”
“A nap?” said Ada. “In the MORNING?”“Even tablets get sleepy,” said Papa.Shhh… the tablet is sleeping.
So what shall we do?
Ada found the blocks.One, two, three… a tower!Four, five, six… taller than Biscuit!Biscuit held very, very still.
Seven… eight… CRASH!Biscuit said, WOOF!Ada said, “AGAIN!”So they built it again. And again. And AGAIN.
Outside: puddles!One with a duck in it!On went the yellow boots.
SPLISH! went the small one.SPLASH! went the middle one.SPLOOOSH! went the big one…all over Papa.Papa laughed. And he jumped in too!
Back inside, Ada peeled offher wet boots and tiptoedto the shelf.Tip… tip… tip…
The tablet was still asleep.It snored a teeny-tiny snore:zzz-bip… zzz-bip…Shhh… the tablet is sleeping.
So what shall we do?
A big, empty box!“That’s not a box,” said Ada.“That’s a ROCKET!”
She gave it windows.She gave it wings.She gave it a purple top,just like the tablet’s nightcap.WOOF! said Biscuit. (That means “Me too!”)
“Ten, nine, eight…” counted Ada.Biscuit held on tight.“…three, two, ONE!”WHOOOOSH!
Past the moon. Past the stars.Past a planet made entirely of socks.Biscuit was the co-pilot.He was very good at barking at comets.
Back on Earth,Ada’s tummy rumbled like a rocket.“Pancakes?” asked Papa.“PANCAKES!” said Ada.
Ada cracked the egg (mostly into the bowl).She stirred and stirred. Papa flipped.Flip! Flop! Plop!“That one looks like the moon,” said Ada.
After pancakes, Ada peeked at the shelf.The tablet had rolled over.Its nightcap had flopped.But it was still asleep.
Shhh… the tablet is sleeping.
So what shall we do?“Let’s go find some stories,” said Papa.
The library had books about dinosaurs,books about rockets,and one about a dog in boots.
“Welcome!” said Ms. Rosa the librarian.“Sleepy stories live here.”“Just like the tablet!” whispered Ada.She picked a book about a growly bear.
That night, Papa read the bear book.Ada did the growly voices.GRRR… GRRR… YAWWWN.
“Best part of today?” asked Papa.“The crash. The splash. The rocket.The pancakes. The library…”Ada yawned a big bear yawn.“All of it.”
Down the hall, in its cozy nightcap,the tablet slept on,dreaming a quiet, happy dream.
And snug in her bed,with Biscuit at her feet,Ada slept too.Shhh… everybody is sleeping.
What a day. What a PLAY day.
In the morning, the tablet woke up and stretched.“Good morning!” said Ada.“Wait till I tell you about my day…”Biscuit said, WOOF!
The End
Talk about it
For grown-ups: after reading, try a few of these. Pause, wait, and let your child answer. There are no wrong answers.
  1. 1If you had a whole day off, what would you do first?
  2. 2Crash, splash, rocket, pancakes, library… which part would you do again?
  3. 3What could a big box turn into at our house?
  4. 4Let’s say the sleepy part together: “Shhh…” Now you be Biscuit!
  5. 5If we cooked together, what would you like to make?
  6. 6Blast off slowly: crouch for “Ten, nine…”, then stretch up tall on “ONE!”
Read it again, and… pause before “do?” and let your child fill it in. Say what you see (“Biscuit is jumping!”), repeat and add one word (“Splash!” becomes “Big splash!”), and follow your child’s lead. Shout, whisper, sign or point along to the “Shhh…” and the “WOOF!” A sign, a point or a tap on a talker (a device a child uses to talk) counts too, and a talker never takes a day off.
Talk, sing and read in the language you know best. Every language counts.
