Grown-up guide · 2 of 2

The cards are the start of a conversation

A routine card is something to talk about together, not a replacement for talking. A card is an invitation, never a test. Start with the first three ideas; add the others when you like. There's nothing to get right.

Shoes on

Say what you see

"Shoes on! Blue shoes." Name the card as your child touches it.

Brush teeth

Pause and wait

"First teeth, then…" and wait a few seconds. A point, a look or a sound is an answer.

Bath time

Repeat and add one word

Your child says "bath." You say "warm bath." One word more than they said.

Puzzle

Offer a choice

Hold up two play cards: "Blocks or puzzle?" Let them pick and put it on the chart.

Feed the pet

Follow their lead

If they grab the dog card, talk about the dog. The plan can wait ten seconds.

Sing a song

Sing and gesture

Make up a tidy-up tune or a wave for "all done." Same song, same moment, every day.

Every language counts

Talk, sing and read in the language you know best. Every language counts. Family words, home languages and pet names all belong on these cards. A sign, a point, a tap on a card, even a "no": it all counts as communicating.

If interest fades

Put the cards away for a week, then bring back just two. Let your child choose which routine gets a chart, or hand them the job of moving the cards. Most children love 2–3 of these; that's normal.

Printer settings

Print at 100% / Actual size, never "Fit to page", so cards stay 2.2 in (5.6 cm). White cardstock, 65–110 lb (176–300 gsm). Landscape pages turn by themselves in most printers; if not, choose "Auto-rotate".

Your two files

This is the Low-ink file. The Color file has the same cards and charts in Rainbow, Soft and Navy. Both come in US Letter and A4.

What you can type

Open this PDF in free Adobe Acrobat Reader (computer or phone). You can type labels on blank, word-free and photo cards, chart titles and names. Accents work (á, ñ, ü); for other alphabets, write by hand. Colors and pictures can't be changed.

Laminate, stick and store

Make them last for years

1

Print on cardstock

White cardstock, 65–110 lb (176–300 gsm), at 100%. Let ink dry a few minutes.

2

Laminate the sheet

3–5 mil pouches. Laminate the full page, then cut. No laminator? Wide clear packing tape or a page protector works.

3

Cut with a sealed edge

Leave about 1/8 in (3 mm) of sealed plastic and round the corners so they're soft in little hands.

4

Dots, ages 3+

Rough (hook) dots on chart slots, soft (loop) dots on card backs. 3/4 in (19 mm) coin dots fit best.

5

Under 3: no dots

Lay cards on top of the chart, drop finished cards in the All done pocket, or slide the chart into a page protector and tuck cards in.

6

Store and reuse

Storage labels go on pouches or envelopes. Laminated blanks and charts take dry-erase markers.

Safety for little hands

  • A grown-up stays close and keeps the pieces. Routine cards are a together activity, not a toy to leave in the crib or bed.
  • Every card is 2.2 in (5.6 cm) square, bigger than a toilet-paper tube opening, a common rule of thumb for small parts with children under 3. Don't shrink the cards when printing, and don't cut them into smaller pieces.
  • Velcro dots, laminating scraps and loose plastic are small parts. Check dots before each play; remove any that lift. Keep spares out of reach, and skip magnets for any child who still puts things in their mouth.
  • Hang charts low enough to reach without climbing, and away from blind cords. Every card follows our published safety rules.

Size check

Measure the dashed square with a ruler after printing. If it's 2.2 in (5.6 cm) on each side, you printed at the right size and every card is bigger than a toilet-paper tube opening. If it's smaller, reprint at 100% / Actual size.

All agesPhoto-frame cards: print, glue, laminateSimple

Photo cards

For the things only your family has: your front door, the car seat, grandma's house, the family pet.

1. Snap a photo of the real thing, close up and in good light.
2. Print it about 1.6 × 1.4 in (4 × 3.5 cm): a wallet-size photo print trimmed down works, or print the photo on plain paper at that size.
3. Glue it inside the dashed frame with a glue stick and let it dry flat.
4. Label by hand, or type in Acrobat Reader before printing this page. Then laminate and cut.
