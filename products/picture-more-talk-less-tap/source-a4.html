Quick start

  1. Print and cut the tower blocks (pages 6–9) and one set of prompt cards. About 20 minutes, once; a paper trimmer helps. For more than 16 children, print the block pages twice.
  2. Put up the poster low, at children’s eye level. Blocks get taped above it: pre-roll tape loops so children can stick their own, and you add the high ones.
  3. Read the teacher script once (page 4).
  4. Play one round at circle time. Count the tower together.

No time to cut? Project the slides and draw the tower on the board, one block per turn. You can play today.

How the game works

Every time a child asks, comments, adds one idea or shows they listened, a block goes on the class tower. Everyone builds one tower together. There are no winners, no points and no blocks taken away.

ADD ONE more idea to what a friend said

Before you start

Printing

  • Print at 100% / actual size so the cards line up. Choose US Letter or A4 to match your paper.
  • Cards and blocks: white cardstock (about 65–110 lb / 176–300 gsm) lasts longest. Plain paper works for a quick try.
  • Laminate the blocks and prompt cards if you will play all year, then round the corners.
  • Save ink: print the script, trackers and family pages in grayscale. Only the blocks and cards need color.
  • Print shop: ask for “single-sided, full color, cardstock, no scaling”. Cut on the dashed lines.
  • Store each card set in its own envelope or zip bag, labeled with its color.

Ages and groups

  • Ages 3–4: start with COMMENT and LISTEN only. Rounds of 3–5 minutes. You read the cards aloud.
  • Ages 5–7: use all four blocks and the prompt cards. Rounds of 8–10 minutes.
  • Works for a whole class in a circle, a small group of 4–6, or pairs.
  • The picture on every card carries its meaning, so pre-readers can play too.

Every way of talking counts

  • Spoken words, signing, pointing, a picture card, a communication device, a nod or a thumbs-up all earn a block.
  • Children may take their turn in the language they know best.
  • “Pass” is always OK. A child who passes can still earn a LISTEN block.
  • Count the class tower, never individual children. Blocks are never taken away.

Safety

  • An adult leads and supervises every round.
  • Paper blocks and cards are large. If you use real blocks or objects with children under 3 nearby, choose pieces too big to fit through a toilet-paper tube (about 1.25 in / 3.2 cm).
  • Use painter’s tape or large Velcro dots on the wall. Check dots before each play. Keep pushpins, staples and small magnets out of reach.
  • For the Talking Star, use the paper star in this kit or a soft toy. For ball games, roll a soft, large ball along the floor.
  • No balloons for children under 8.
This kit is a set of general classroom play ideas. It is not a program, assessment or screening tool, and it is not professional advice.

Why it works

Children get better at conversation by having lots of conversations: asking, answering, adding on and listening, with grown-ups and friends who wait for them. The tower makes each of those turns visible, so the whole class can see them and cheer. For the youngest, saying something back comes before asking a friend a question, so start there.

Three talk lines for any time

“Tell me more.”
“What happened next?”
“I wonder…”

Most classes love 2–3 of the six variations. Keep your favorites and skip the rest.

Teacher script: your first round

About 10 minutes (3–5 for ages 3–4, page 3) · whole group · say the words in bold quotes; do the gray notes

1

Gather 1 min

Children sit in a circle. The poster is up, the blocks are in a basket, and the Talking Star is in your hand.

“Today we are going to build something by taking turns to talk. It is called a Talk Tower!”

2

Meet the blocks 2 min

Hold up one block at a time.

“Blue means ask: you ask a friend a question. Yellow means comment: you say something back. Green means add one: you add one more idea. Purple means listen: you show you heard your friend.”

3

Show one turn 1 min

Play a turn with a child or another adult so everyone sees it first.

“What did you play today?” (Child answers.) “Me too! I love the sand table.” (Add a yellow block.) “My comment made our tower grow!”

4

Play the round 5 min

Draw a topic card and pass the star. After each turn, the child adds a block, or you add it together.

“Whoever holds the star takes a turn. Everyone else listens, their own way. You can ask, comment, add one, or say pass.”

5

Count and celebrate 1 min

Count together. The last child puts the Talking Star on top. Write the height on the tracker.

“Let’s count our tower: one, two, three… Our tower is ___ blocks tall! We built that together, one turn at a time.”

If this happens…

Everyone talks at once

“Tower wobble! One voice at a time. Who has the star?” Hold up the wobble sign.

A child does not want a turn

“Pass is OK. You can point, nod or show me. Want to earn a listen block?”

One child talks a lot

“Great idea! Hold it for the next round. Let’s hear from someone who hasn’t had a turn.”

The tower is small today

“Every block counts. We will build more tomorrow.”

A child needs to move

Let them stand or rock nearby. A moving body can still listen.

The blocks run out

Reuse them each round, or add tally marks on the board.

Next time, try a variation from pages 18–19. The bonus story More Talk, Less Tap is a fun way to start on day one.

Stick every name block in Ready for a turn. After a child’s turn, they move their own name across. When every name has moved, the round is done: move them all back and start again.

Circle-time variations

Six more ways to build the tower. Each one works with the same blocks, cards and rules, needs nothing to buy, and makes no mess.

1

Pass the Talking Star

Whole group · From 36 months · 5–10 min · Prep: none · You need: Talking Star, one topic card

  1. Read the topic card aloud.
  2. Pass the star around the circle. Whoever holds it may ask, comment, add one or pass.
  3. Add a block after every turn and count the tower at the end.

2-minute version, no setup: Skip the topic card. Ask one question and pass the star to three children. One block each.

Make it easier: Everyone finishes the same starter: “I like…”

Make it harder: Each turn connects to the friend before: “Add one to what Maya said.”

2

Add-One Story Tower

Whole group · From 48 months · 10 min · Prep: none · You need: Green blocks (for a whole class, print page 8 twice)

  1. Start a silly story: “Once there was a dog who wanted to…”
  2. Each child adds one thing to the story, and a green block goes on the tower.
  3. At the end, retell the whole story together, one block at a time.

2-minute version, no setup: Say one story starter. Three children add one word each, then say the whole line together.

Make it easier: Add just one word each: “a big… red… bus”.

Make it harder: Add a problem, then ask the circle how to fix it.

3

Ask Me One More

Pairs · From 48 months · 5 min · Prep: none · You need: Nothing at all

  1. One partner shares something: “I have a cat.”
  2. The other listens, then asks one more question: “What is its name?”
  3. Swap. Each pair adds a blue block to the class tower.

2-minute version, no setup: One pair shows the class: one shares, one asks one more question. Everyone cheers their way: clap, wave or a silent cheer.

Make it easier: Hand the asker an ASK card to read or point to.

Make it harder: Try for three questions in a row on the same topic.

Circle-time variations (continued)

4

Comment Catch

Small group · From 42 months · 5 min · Prep: find a soft ball · You need: A large soft ball

  1. Sit in a small circle. One child shares a sentence about the topic.
  2. Roll the ball along the floor to a friend.
  3. The catcher makes a comment about what they heard, then shares their own sentence and rolls on.

2-minute version, no setup: No ball: point to a friend instead of rolling. Two or three turns.

Make it easier: Keep a COMMENT card in the middle to point to.

Make it harder: The catcher starts with the sharer’s name: “Leo, that is funny!”

5

Listening Tower

Whole group · From 42 months · 5 min · Prep: none · You need: Purple blocks (for a whole class, print page 9 twice)

  1. A child shares one thing about their day.
  2. The next child says back one thing they heard, then shares their own.
  3. Every say-back earns a purple block. A calm way to end a busy day.

2-minute version, no setup: You share one thing about your day. Two children say back what they heard.

Make it easier: The grown-up says it back first, then invites a child to try.

Make it harder: Say back two things you heard.

6

Mystery Bag Talk

Small group · From 36 months · 10 min · Prep: 1 min to fill the bag · You need: A cloth bag and one classroom object

  1. Hide one object in the bag. Pick something too big to fit through a toilet-paper tube.
  2. Children ask questions to guess it: “Is it soft?” Each question adds a blue block.
  3. Reveal it, then everyone comments or adds one idea about it.

2-minute version, no setup: No bag: hold one classroom object behind your back and take three guesses.

Make it easier: Answer with a yes or no, and let children feel the bag.

Make it harder: The child who guesses hides the next object and answers the questions.

Keep it a celebration. Count the class tower, not individual turns. No winners, no prizes, and no child is made to talk before they are ready.

From our classroom to your home

Play Talk Tower at home

Dear families,

This week our class played Talk Tower, a talk game where every question, comment and idea adds a block to our class tower. Our tower grew to   blocks! Here is how to play at home. You need no screens and nothing special.

How to play (5 minutes)

  1. Pick a moment: dinner, the car, bath time or a walk.
  2. Pick a topic: food, animals, today, a silly “what if”.
  3. Take turns: ask a question, say something back, or add one more idea.
  4. Build: every turn adds a block. Use cups, spoons, socks or cereal boxes, or color a block on the sheet we sent home.
  5. Count your tower together at the end.

Tips for grown-ups

  • Pause and wait. Count to five in your head after you ask.
  • Say what you see. “You’re smiling! Is that a funny idea?”
  • Repeat and add one word. “A dog!” “A big dog!”
  • Follow their lead. Talk about what your child already loves.
  • Every way counts. Pointing, signing, gestures and a tap on a talking device are turns too. A talking device is a child’s voice, not screen time.
  • Use your home language. Talk, sing and read in the language you know best.

Safety: an adult plays along. If babies or toddlers are nearby, use objects too big to fit through a toilet-paper tube.

Bonus: the read-aloud story

More Talk, Less Tap is a 32-page picture-book story that comes free with this kit. Room 5 builds a Talk Tower, one question, joke and idea at a time, until everybody talks at once and… CRASH! Quiet Sam has the idea that saves the day: take turns.

One difference: in the story, yellow blocks are for jokes and laughs. In the game, yellow means comment, saying something back to a friend. Laughing at a friend’s joke still counts! And the “tap” in the title? It is the rain on the window and Bubbles the fish. A tap on a talking device is talk, and it always earns a block.

File: Talk-Tower-Story-Read-Aloud.pdf (square pages). Project it for the group, or print it one or two pages per sheet.

Before

  • Show the cover. “What do you think a Talk Tower is?”
  • Point to the four blocks. Can anyone guess what each one means?

During

  • Invite everyone to join in on “CLACK!” and “Up it goes!”, loud or whisper-quiet.
  • Pause at the crash. “Uh-oh. What should Room 5 do?”
  • Give Sam’s quiet idea a moment of wait time.

After

  • “Which block would you add first?”
  • Play your first Talk Tower round (teacher script, page 4).
  • The story’s back pages have more talk starters and games.
