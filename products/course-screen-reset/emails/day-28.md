# Tough days, sick days, travel days

**A planned change for a hard day is fine. Say it out loud, and go back to your usual rhythm the next day.**

Some days the plan will bend. A sick child on the couch. A long flight. A day when you are the one who is unwell. A work deadline. On those days, screens may fill more of the day.

That’s okay. A planned change for a hard day is not a failure, and it doesn’t undo anything. Two things make it work.

Say it out loud. “You’re sick today, so we’re having a cozy movie day on the couch together. Tomorrow we go back to our usual plan.” When it’s spoken as a special exception, children understand it’s not the new normal.

Go back the next day. No “making up for it”, no less screen time as a correction. Just the usual spot, in its usual place.

Remember: a sick day isn’t about behavior, so this doesn’t break the rule that screens don’t grow or shrink with chores or behavior. It’s about what your family needs that day.

Quiet plays help on sick days too: stories, drawing in bed, a basket of books, watching the rain.

**Today’s one small step:** Write your “tough day plan” in the workbook: what a cozy day looks like, and the words for going back.

## Today’s play: Cozy tray

*From 2 years · 2-min prep · No mess · Short play · You need: a tray or baking sheet, paper, crayons, a book, a small toy*

Set up a tray your child can use on the couch or in bed: paper, crayons, a book, one small toy. Sit nearby and talk about what they draw or find.

**Talk while you play (Say what you see):** “You drew a big circle. A big round sun. A cozy sun!”

- **Make it easier:** One book and one teddy on the tray.
- **Make it harder:** Preschoolers draw a “sick day newspaper” with the family’s news while you write the words.
- **Tired-grown-up version (2 minutes):** Put the tray down and rest nearby.
- **Safety:** For under-3s, everything on the tray must be too big to fit through a toilet-paper tube. Follow your pediatrician’s advice when your child is ill.

## Plain words for: On a sick or travel day

> “You’re sick today, so it’s a cozy movie day. Tomorrow we go back to our usual plan.”
>
> “Long flight today! Shows after the first snack.”

Naming the exception keeps it an exception.
