# Look back: what did they ask for again?

**The plays your child asked for again are your family’s real plan. Write them down.**

Nearly there. Today, look back over the month.

Open your tracker and your check-ins. Circle every play your child asked for again. Most children love two or three plays far more than the rest, and they return to them over and over. Those are your family’s favorites, and they’re the heart of your plan.

Then look at the tricky moments. Which ones got easier? The end of screen time? The morning scramble? The hour before dinner? Which words from the scripts did you actually use?

And look at yourself. What surprised you? Maybe it was how much talking happened once there was a little more room for it. Maybe it was how little your child minded, once the screen spot was steady.

Whatever you notice, write it down. You’ll use it tomorrow.

Every child talks, plays and grows on their own timeline. If you have questions about your child’s development, talk with your pediatrician.

**Today’s one small step:** Circle your child’s top three plays and your three most useful scripts.

## Today’s play: Favorites replay

*From 12 months · No prep · A little mess · Short play · You need: whatever the favorite play needs*

Remind your child of two plays from this month and ask, “Which one shall we play again?” Play it their way, for as long as they like.

**Talk while you play (Offer a choice):** “Sock basketball or blanket hideout? (wait) Hideout!”

- **Make it easier:** Hold up a sock and a blanket so your toddler can point.
- **Make it harder:** Big kids rank the month’s plays and make a top-ten list.
- **Tired-grown-up version (2 minutes):** Play the two-minute version of their favorite.
- **Safety:** Follow the safety note on the original play.

## Plain words for: Asking your child what they liked

> “What was the most fun thing we played this month?”
>
> “What should we play more of?”

Children are experts on their own fun. Their answer is your plan.
