Your family’s plan

Write a short, kind plan you can keep: your screen spot, your favorite plays and your words for tricky moments.

Day 30. You did it: thirty days of adding play and talk to real family life.

Today, write your family’s plan on one page. Keep it short enough to stick on the fridge.

Our screen spot: when, where, what and how it ends.

Our favorite plays: the two or three your child keeps asking for, plus one new one to try next month.

Our words for tricky moments: the lines that worked for the end of screen time, for waiting, and for “I’m bored”.

Our phone parking spot and our one phone-free window.

Our tough-day plan.

Then celebrate. Print the certificate, let your child decorate it and put it somewhere everyone can see.

Look at the plan again in a month and adjust. Families change, children grow, and the plan should grow with them. This month isn’t a finish line; it’s a rhythm you now know how to find.

Thank you for letting us be part of your month.

Today’s play: Family play parade

From 12 months · 2-min prep · A little mess · Short play
You need: pots and spoons, hats, anything that makes a parade

Celebrate with a parade around your home: bang pots, wear hats, sing your family songs from this month. Each child leads for one lap. Finish by putting your certificate on the fridge.

Talk while you play · Sing and gesture
“Left, right, bang, bang! Who leads next?”

Make it easier: Carry your toddler and bang a spoon together.
Make it harder: Big kids write a family “anthem” and conduct the band.
Tired-grown-up version (2 minutes): March your fingers across the table and hum.

Safety: March on a clear floor, away from stairs, and keep the noise kind to little ears.

Plain words for: Explaining the family plan going forward

“This is our family’s plan. Screens after nap, and lots of play.”

“We’ll look at it again next month and change what we need to.”

A plan that can change is a plan that lasts.
