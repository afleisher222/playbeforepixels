# Repeat and add one

**When your child says or shows something, say it back and add one more word or idea.**

Today’s move grows your child’s words from the ones they already use.

When your child says or shows something, say it back, and add one more word or idea. If your toddler says “Ball!”, you say “Big ball!” If your preschooler says “The dog is running,” you say “The dog is running fast! He wants the stick.”

This does two things. It tells your child “I heard you”, which makes them want to keep talking. And it shows them the next step, just a little further along.

If your child says a word in a way that isn’t quite right, don’t correct them. Just say it back the right way, warmly: “Nana!” “Yes, banana. A yellow banana.” They hear the model without feeling wrong.

For big kids, it becomes “tell me more”: “You had a weird day. What was the weirdest part?”

Talk in the language you know best. The richest talk is the talk that feels natural to you.

**Today’s one small step:** Every time your child says or shows something today, try to add one word.

## Today’s play: Add-one story

*From 2½ years · No prep · No mess · Short play · You need: a picture book or family photo*

Look at a picture together. Your child says one thing about it, you repeat it and add one more thing. Keep going back and forth to build a silly story.

**Talk while you play (Repeat and add one):** “Cat.” “A sleepy cat!” “Sleepy cat in a hat!”

- **Make it easier:** Name one picture and add one word: “Dog.” “Big dog.”
- **Make it harder:** Big kids build a story chain, one sentence each, with a twist every third turn.
- **Tired-grown-up version (2 minutes):** Say one word each about the picture and stop there.
- **Safety:** Use sturdy board books with young children; paper pages can tear into mouth-sized bits.

## Plain words for: Instead of correcting

> Child: “Doggy go.” You: “Yes! The doggy’s going. Going home.”
>
> Child: “I goed.” You: “You went! You went down the big slide.”

Say it back the right way, warmly. No “say it properly”.
