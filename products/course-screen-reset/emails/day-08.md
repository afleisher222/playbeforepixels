*Day 8 of 30 · Play comes first*

# Play first, screens in their spot

**Put a short play before the screen spot so play gets the fresh energy of the day.**

This week, play moves to the front.

Look at your screen spot. What happens right before it? If the answer is “waiting for it”, try putting a short play there instead. For example: after nap, ten minutes of blocks together, then shows on the couch.

Please hear this clearly: this is about the order of the day, not about earning. The screen spot happens either way, at the same time. If the play goes badly, the screen spot still happens. If the play goes brilliantly, the screen spot is still the same length. Play isn’t the price of a screen. It’s just what comes first.

Why this helps: children often play with more energy and focus before a screen than right after one. And a play that’s already going sometimes carries right through the screen spot, because your child is too busy to notice the time.

Keep it short and easy. A few minutes counts. You’re building a rhythm, not a school timetable.

**Today’s one small step:** Put a short play right before today’s screen spot. Keep the spot the same either way.

## Today’s play: Sock basketball

*From 18 months · No prep · No mess · Short play · You need: 4–6 pairs of rolled-up socks, a laundry basket*

Roll socks into balls. Put the basket a few steps away and take turns throwing. Cheer every throw, in or out. Move the basket closer or further as you go.

**Talk while you play (Repeat and add one):** “Throw! … In! Two in! Big throw!”

- **Make it easier:** Put the basket right in front of your toddler and drop the socks in.
- **Make it harder:** Big kids invent scoring rules: bounce shots, backwards shots, trick shots.
- **Tired-grown-up version (2 minutes):** Sit on the couch and toss socks into the basket from where you are.
- **Safety:** Roll each pair into one big ball; single small items stay away from under-3s.

## Plain words for: “Can I have screens after this?”

> “Yes. Screens are after nap, like always.”
>
> “Right now it’s play time. Your throw!”

A calm yes about the spot takes the worry out of the question. The spot is dependable.
