Big feelings when screens end

When a child melts down as screen time ends, stay close, name the feeling and keep the ending kindly in place.

Some days the ending won’t be smooth, no matter what you do. Your child might cry, shout or throw themselves on the floor. That’s a normal response to disappointment, especially when children are young or tired.

Here’s what helps most: stay calm and stay close. You don’t need to fix the feeling or talk your child out of it. You just need to keep the limit in place while the feeling passes.

Name the feeling in a few words: “You’re so mad. You wanted another show.” Naming it helps children feel understood, even while you hold the line.

Keep your words few. Long explanations are hard to hear when someone is upset. Say the plan once: “The tablet is asleep. It will be here tomorrow after nap.”

Some children need quiet and a little space first; stay nearby. When the storm settles, reconnect: a hug if they want one, a drink of water, the landing activity you planned. There’s no need for a talk about it later, unless your child wants one.

If big feelings about screens feel constant or overwhelming, or you’re worried about your child, talk with your pediatrician. You don’t have to handle it alone.

Today’s one small step: Practice today’s script out loud once, so the words are ready when you need them.

Today’s play: Blow out the candles

From 2 years · No prep · No mess · Quick play
You need: Nothing but you

Hold up your fingers as birthday candles. Your child takes a big breath and blows each one out, and you fold it down. Relight them and go again. Slow breaths help bodies calm down.

Talk while you play · Sing and gesture
“Big breath in… and blow! One candle out. Four more!”

Make it easier: One candle, one blow, and a big cheer.
Make it harder: Preschoolers blow a tissue across the table with slow, steady breaths.
Tired-grown-up version (2 minutes): Just breathe slowly together with a hand on your tummies.

Safety: Use pretend candles only. No real flames, and no balloons for under-8s.

Plain words for: A meltdown when the screen turns off

“You’re so mad. It’s hard when a show ends.”

“I’m right here. The tablet will be back tomorrow after nap.”

Name the feeling, keep the limit, stay close. That’s all. No lecture, no threats.
