# Pause and wait

**Leaving a real pause after you speak gives children time to take their turn.**

This week, one simple talk move each day. They all fit into the play you’re already doing.

Today: pause and wait.

Grown-ups talk fast and fill silences, especially when we’re busy. But children, especially young ones, often need a few seconds to get a word, a sound or a gesture ready. If we jump in too soon, we take their turn without meaning to.

So: say something, then stop. Count to five slowly in your head. Look at your child with an expectant, friendly face, like you’re waiting for the punchline. Often, something comes: a look, a point, a sound, a word.

Whatever comes, answer it as if it were the best thing anyone has said all day. That’s a turn. That’s conversation.

The pause works in songs too, in any language: “Twinkle, twinkle, little…” then wait. Or in games: “Ready, set…” and wait for your child to say or sign “Go!”

It feels strange at first. Keep going. It may turn out to be the move that surprises you most.

**Today’s one small step:** Use a five-second pause at least three times today.

## Today’s play: Ready, set… go!

*From 12 months · No prep · No mess · Quick play · You need: a toy car or a ball, a book or board for a ramp*

Lean a book on a cushion to make a ramp. Hold the car at the top and say “Ready, set…” and wait. When your child looks, sounds or says “Go!”, let it roll. Cheer and do it again.

**Talk while you play (Pause and wait):** “Ready… set… (wait) GO!”

- **Make it easier:** Roll a ball back and forth, pausing before each push.
- **Make it harder:** Big kids build a longer ramp and measure how far each car rolls.
- **Tired-grown-up version (2 minutes):** Play “ready, set, go” with tickles on the couch.
- **Safety:** For under-3s, toy cars and balls must be too big to fit through a toilet-paper tube.

## Plain words for: When your child doesn’t answer

> (Wait. Smile. Count to five in your head.)
>
> “You’re thinking. I’ll wait.” Then answer any look, point or sound.

No pressure, no testing. The pause is an invitation, not a quiz.
