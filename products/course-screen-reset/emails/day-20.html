Sing, gesture and your own language

Songs, rhymes and gestures make words easy to join in with, in any language.

Today: sing.

You don’t need a good voice. Children love their grown-ups’ singing, off-key and all. Songs slow words down, repeat them and add rhythm, which makes them easier to join in with. Gestures (clapping, waving, the actions in action songs) give children a way to join before they have the words.

Pick two or three songs and sing them often: at bath time, in the car, while you wait. Repeat, repeat, repeat. Then pause before a familiar word and let your child fill it in with a sound, a sign or a word.

Sing in the language you know best, or in every language your family speaks. Lullabies from your childhood count. Songs you make up about brushing teeth count. It all counts.

Use gestures when you talk, too: point, wave, nod, show “big” with your arms. Signs and gestures are real communication, and many children use them on the way to words or alongside them.

Today’s one small step: Choose two family songs. Sing each at least twice today.

Today’s play: Song basket

From 12 months · 2-min prep · No mess · Short play
You need: a basket, 4–5 objects that stand for songs (a toy duck, a spoon, a star cut from paper)

Put a few objects in a basket, each one standing for a song: a duck for a duck song, a star for a star song, in whatever language your family sings. Your child pulls one out and you sing that song together, with actions.

Talk while you play · Sing and gesture
“A star! Twinkle, twinkle, little… (wait) …star!”

Make it easier: Just two objects and two songs, sung again and again.
Make it harder: Big kids make up new verses or a song about the family dog.
Tired-grown-up version (2 minutes): Hum a lullaby with your child on your lap.

Safety: For under-3s, every object must be too big to fit through a toilet-paper tube. Paper stars stay with grown-ups.

Plain words for: When you feel silly singing

(To yourself) “My child doesn’t need a good singer. They need me.”

“I don’t know all the words. Let’s make them up!”

Your voice is the one your child most wants to hear.
