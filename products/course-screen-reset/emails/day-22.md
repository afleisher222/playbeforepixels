# Waiting without a screen

**A few pocket plays that need nothing at all turn waiting rooms, lines and restaurants into talk time.**

This week we take the plan out into the world, starting with waiting.

Waiting is hard for young children. In doctors’ offices, restaurant booths and long lines, a phone can feel like the only thing that works. Sometimes it is, and that’s okay. But it helps to have a few pocket plays ready first.

Pocket plays need nothing but you: I spy, finger puppets, clapping games, “What’s in my pocket?”, drawing on a napkin, counting red things, guessing what the waiter will bring. Keep them short and silly and switch often.

A few things make waiting easier. Feed hungry children first (a snack in your bag solves a lot). Tell your child what will happen: “We’ll wait, then the doctor will look in your ears.” And lower your expectations: wriggly is normal.

Big kids can bring a notebook and pencil for waiting. Tic-tac-toe, drawing games and word games travel anywhere.

**Today’s one small step:** Pick three pocket plays to keep in your back pocket. Write them on your phone’s lock screen or a card in your bag.

## Today’s play: I spy with colors

*From 2½ years · No prep · No mess · Quick play · You need: Nothing but you*

Say “I spy with my little eye something that is… blue!” and let your child look and guess. Give clues if needed. Then it’s your child’s turn to spy.

**Talk while you play (Pause and wait):** “I spy something… blue! (wait) Yes, the door!”

- **Make it easier:** Point and name: “I see a blue door. Can you see it?”
- **Make it harder:** Big kids use first letters, shapes or “something that’s used for…” clues.
- **Tired-grown-up version (2 minutes):** Count the red things you can see, together.
- **Safety:** Keep children seated or holding hands in busy public places.

## Plain words for: “Can I have your phone?” while waiting

> “My phone is staying in my bag. Let’s play I spy until the food comes.”
>
> “You can pick: I spy or napkin drawing?”

A clear, friendly no plus a choice. If you do decide to hand over the phone, that’s your call; say so plainly.
