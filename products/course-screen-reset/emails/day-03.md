# Give screens a steady spot

**A screen spot that happens at the same time, in the same place, with the same ending, cuts most daily negotiating.**

Here is the single biggest change in this program: give screens a steady spot in your day.

A steady spot means screens happen at roughly the same time each day, in the same place, and end the same way. For example: “Shows are after nap, on the couch, for two episodes, and then we go outside.” Or for a big kid: “Screen time is 4:30 to 5:15, in the living room, and ends when the kitchen timer rings.”

Why this works: when children know when screens come, they stop asking all day. The spot becomes like lunch. It isn’t a prize, and it isn’t a threat.

That last part matters. In this program, screens are never earned and never taken away as a punishment. They don’t grow when chores are done or shrink after a hard morning. The spot simply stays put. Linking screens to behavior tends to make them feel more valuable, and turns every day into a negotiation.

Choose a spot that fits your real life. If you need 30 minutes to cook dinner, that may be your spot. That is a sensible plan, not a failure.

The American Academy of Pediatrics (2016) suggests families make a media plan, choose shows with care, and watch together when they can. Your screen spot is the start of that plan.

## Today’s play: Stop and go dance

*From 15 months · No prep · No mess · Quick play · You need: music, or your own singing*

Play a song and dance together. When you stop the music, everyone freezes. Hold the pause a little longer each time, then start again with a big “Go!”

**Talk while you play (Pause and wait):** “Ready… set… (wait) GO!”

- **Make it easier:** Hold your toddler and sway, then freeze together.
- **Make it harder:** Big kids control the music, or freeze in a shape you call out: tall, tiny, wobbly.
- **Tired-grown-up version (2 minutes):** Sit on the floor and do stop-and-go clapping.
- **Safety:** Clear a space away from sharp furniture corners and stairs.

## Plain words for: Telling your child about the screen spot

> “Here’s our new plan. Shows are after nap, on the couch. Two shows, then outside.”
>
> “Every day. Same time, same place.”

Say it before the first day, when nobody is upset. Repeat it the same way each time.
