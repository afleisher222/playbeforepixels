Why busy-book play?

Little ones learn words from the people who talk and play with them. A busy book gives the two of you something to point at, name and move around, so the talk comes easily. You don’t need a plan or a script; the pages give you one.

2-minute setup

  1. Print one page. Cutting can wait.
  2. Sit side by side or with your child on your lap.
  3. Let your child touch, point and choose first.
  4. Say the “talk while you play” line, then pause.
  5. Stop while it’s still fun. Short is perfect.

Most children love 2–3 of these

You don’t need all 74. Most children find two or three favorites and want them again and again. That’s not boring for them: repeating is how play grows. Rotate a few pages each week and keep the rest for later.

Three talk lines that work on any page

Say what you see

“Big red apple!”

Name what your child is looking at, in a few words.

Pause and wait

“Ready, set… (wait)”

Count to five in your head. Give your child room to take a turn.

Add one word

“Duck.” → “Yellow duck!”

Repeat what your child says and add one word.

Every turn counts

Talk, sing and read in the language you know best. A sign, a point, a look, a sound or a device tap all count as a turn. There’s no right answer on any page: if your child calls the frog a “duck,” say “A green frog! Ribbit!” and keep playing.

Safety first

Our play safety rules

Every page in this book follows these rules. Please read them once before you start.

The toilet-paper-tube test

For children under 3, nothing small enough to fit through a toilet-paper tube (about 1.25 in / 3.2 cm across). Every cut piece in this book is 2 in (5.1 cm) or bigger on its shortest side, and the pieces for 1–2 years are 2.5 in (6.4 cm) or bigger.

Grown-up keeps the pieces

Count pieces out and back in. Store them in a pouch or envelope that the grown-up keeps. Throw away any piece that tears, bends, gets wet or peels.

Always together

Every activity is played with a grown-up right there. Read together and keep paper and pieces away from mouths.

Velcro rules

No loose velcro dots for under-3s: for 1–2 and 2–3 pages, lay pieces on top. For 3–5 pages velcro is optional: check dots before each play and keep pieces away from younger children.

Laminated edges

Leave a small border when you cut laminated pieces and round the corners so edges stay soft.

Crayons and pens

Dry-erase crayons are for the 3–5 pages, with a grown-up. The grown-up keeps caps, which are small.

Pretend food only

The food pages are pictures. No real food is part of any activity, and none of the pictured foods are small, hard or round snacks.

Nothing else to add

No balloons, beads, buttons, coins, strings or cords are used or needed anywhere in this book.

Real toys? A few pages suggest a real toy (a ball, a toy car, a box). For under-3s, any real toy must pass the tube test and have no small parts that come off. If you’re ever unsure, leave it out: the page works without it.

Assembly guide

Build your busy book three ways

Pick the one that fits your week. You can start with the quick one today and upgrade later.

Quick: no laminator

  1. Print pages on cardstock (or plain paper).
  2. Slide each page into a clear sheet protector.
  3. Keep pages in a 1–1.5 in 3-ring binder.
  4. Pieces go in a zip pouch clipped to the binder.

About 15 minutes for 10 pages.

Classic: laminated

  1. Print on cardstock and laminate (3–5 mil pouches).
  2. Cut pieces with a small clear border; round corners.
  3. Punch holes and use binder rings or a binder.
  4. One labeled pouch per piece sheet.

Pieces last for months of play.

Velcro: ages 3–5 only

  1. Laminate first, then add dots.
  2. Soft (loop) dot on the page, scratchy (hook) dot on the piece.
  3. Press firmly; let dots set overnight.
  4. Check dots before each play. No velcro for under-3s.

Use only for the 3–5 pages.

Handy supplies

  • Cardstock, 65–110 lb (176–300 gsm)
  • Scissors or a paper trimmer (straight cuts only)
  • Sheet protectors or a laminator (optional)
  • Binder or binder rings, and zip pouches
  • Velcro dots for the 3–5 pages (optional)

Organize by age

Use one binder cover per child or per age band (4 colors on pages 13–16). Tape a pouch label to each pouch (page 18). Keep 5–8 pages in the binder and rotate the rest each week with the planner on page 124.

Laminating & velcro tips

Make it last

Laminating

  • Use 3–5 mil pouches. Thicker pouches make stiffer pieces that little hands grip easily.
  • Laminate the whole sheet first, then cut. Leave about 1/8 in (3 mm) of clear border so the seal holds.
  • Round every corner with scissors or a corner rounder.
  • No laminator? Clear sheet protectors work for pages; packing tape over both sides works for a few pieces.
  • Wipe pages with a damp cloth. Let them dry flat.

Velcro (3–5 pages only)

  • Soft “loop” dots go on the page; scratchy “hook” dots go on the back of pieces.
  • Put the dot in the middle of the slot so pieces sit straight.
  • Press for 30 seconds and let dots set overnight before play.
  • Check dots before each play. Throw away any piece whose dot lifts at the edge.
  • Never on 1–2 or 2–3 pages: lay those pieces on top instead.

Dry-erase

Mazes and road pages work with a dry-erase crayon on laminated pages. Wipe with a dry cloth. The grown-up keeps caps.

Storage

One zip pouch per piece sheet, labeled with its page number. Every piece also shows its page in the corner.

Save ink

Print the Low-ink file for pages you want to color, and for piece sheets you’ll laminate anyway.

Worn out? That means it was loved. Reprint just that page: the files are yours to print again for your own home.

Printing tips

Print only what you need

Pick your file

  • US Letter (8.5 × 11 in) or A4 (210 × 297 mm): same pages, same piece sizes.
  • Color for bright pages. Low-ink for white pages with colorable line art.
  • The make-your-own pages can be typed into in a free PDF reader before printing.

Printer settings

  • Print at 100% / actual size. “Fit to page” shrinks pieces.
  • Single-sided only: pieces and pages need blank backs.
  • Print a page range (for example 19–27) instead of the whole book.

Paper

Cardstock (65–110 lb / 176–300 gsm) for pieces and anything you’ll handle a lot. Plain paper is fine for no-cut pages in sheet protectors.

Home or print shop

Home printers are perfect. For a sturdy set, a local print shop can print on cardstock and laminate; take the file on a USB stick and ask for “actual size.”

Check your size

Quick answers

Questions, answered

What ages is it for?

About 12 months to 5 years, in three bands (1–2, 2–3, 3–5). Every page shows its starting age in months. Move up or down freely.

Do I have to cut everything?

No. 49 activities need no cutting at all. Cut piece sheets when you have ten minutes; each has 12 straight cuts or fewer.

Are the pieces safe for my toddler?

Every piece is 2 in (5.1 cm) or bigger, bigger than a toilet-paper tube. A grown-up plays along, keeps the pieces and checks them each time. No velcro dots for under-3s.

Which file should I print?

US Letter or A4 to match your paper. Color for bright pages; Low-ink for white pages with colorable line art. Print at 100% / actual size.

Can I type into the pages?

Yes: the make-your-own pages, binder covers, labels, planner and certificate have type-in boxes that work in free PDF readers. Type, save, then print.

Do I need a laminator?

No. Sheet protectors or plain cardstock work well. Laminating just makes pieces last longer (tips on page 9).

Is anything shipped?

No, it’s a digital download. You print at home or at a print shop, as many copies as your own family needs.

My child only wants one page. Is that OK?

Completely. Most children love two or three pages and repeat them. Try that page’s “harder” line when it feels easy.

Is this a lesson plan or a program?

No. It’s play for families: pages to talk about together, with no tests or scores. It isn’t medical or professional advice. Questions about development go to your pediatrician.

Can I use it with a group or class?

This copy is for your own home. Child-care and classroom licenses are at playbeforepixels.com/licenses.For a child-care or classroom license, send us a message through the shop.

Can I share it with a friend?

Please share a photo of your busy book, not the files. If a friend would like a copy, they can buy their own; it keeps new pages coming.

A file won’t open on my phone

Download on a computer or tablet if you can. Help with downloads and printing: playbeforepixels.com/help.Etsy downloads work best in a web browser on a computer or tablet (the app can’t download files). Send us a message through the shop if you’re stuck.
