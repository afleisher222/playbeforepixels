Grown-up guide

How to give a gift of play

A coupon is a promise to play: time together, not a thing to buy. It takes about 2 minutes to set up and works as a gift on its own or tucked in with another gift.

1 · Pick

Choose 3–6 coupons that suit your child’s age. Each shows a starting age. Fill in a blank one with a play your family already loves.

2 · Wrap

Cut them out, add the coupon-book cover and staple the stack at the left edge. Or fold the whole page like a letter.

3 · Reveal

Use the fold card or a reveal card to say what’s inside. Your child hands you a coupon when they want to play it.

Talk while you play

Pause and wait

Say a little, then stop. A look, a sound or a wiggle is an answer.

“Ready, set… (wait) roll!”

Say what you see

Put words to what your child is doing, right as it happens.

“Big bubble… POP!”

Offer a choice

Hold up two things and wait. Pointing counts as an answer.

“Left or right?”

Most children love 2–3 of these and ask for them again and again. Say yes as often as you can, and let the rest wait. Talk, sing and read in the language you know best. A sign, a point or a device tap counts as communicating.

Why coupons?

Young children learn in back-and-forth moments with the people who love them. A coupon puts one of those moments on the calendar. It adds play; it never takes anything away.

A few house rules

Coupons never expire and are never taken back. They are never traded for screen time or sweets, and they are never a reward for good behavior. They are just for fun.

Coupon key · 1–8

Easier, harder, a 2-minute version and a safety line for every coupon

1. A Blanket Fortfrom 18 mo From 18 mo · Blankets, chairs, a book

Easier: One blanket over the table.

Harder: Add a door, a window and a sign.

2-minute: Blanket over your knees; one page of a book.

Safety: Nothing heavy on top; no cords or clips holding the blankets. Keep one side open.

2. A Kitchen Dance Partyfrom 1 yr From 12 mo · Any music, or your singing

Easier: Hold your baby and sway.

Harder: Take turns leading the moves.

2-minute: One song, one twirl.

Safety: Clear the floor first. Bare feet or grippy socks.

3. Pancake Helperfrom 30 mo From 30 mo · Pancake things, a bowl, a spoon

Easier: You stir; the grown-up pours.

Harder: Count the spoonfuls and read the steps together.

2-minute: Stir the bowl ten times together.

Safety: Only the grown-up works at the stove and flips. Hot pan stays at the back. Check allergies.

4. A Walk Where You Leadfrom 1 yr From 12 mo · Shoes and coats

Easier: Stroller or carrier walk; name what you see.

Harder: Draw a map of where we went.

2-minute: Once around the block, or to the window and back.

Safety: Hold hands near roads. Look, don’t taste: berries, nuts and leaves stay out of mouths.

5. Build Anythingfrom 18 mo From 18 mo · Blocks, boxes or cushions

Easier: Stack three, knock them down.

Harder: Build a bridge a toy can drive under.

2-minute: One tower, one crash.

Safety: Under 3: only blocks too big to fit through a toilet-paper tube. Keep towers below chest height.

6. Bubble Timefrom 1 yr From 12 mo · Bubble mix and a wand

Easier: Pop them with one finger.

Harder: Blow your own and count them.

2-minute: Ten bubbles, ten pops.

Safety: A grown-up blows bubbles for children under 3. Wipe up spills so no one slips. Nobody drinks the mix.

7. A Sock Puppet Showfrom 2 yrs From 24 mo · Clean socks, a sofa

Easier: The grown-up’s puppet says hello and tickles.

Harder: Your puppet tells a story with a start and an end.

2-minute: One sock, one “Hello!”

Safety: Socks with nothing sewn or glued on: no buttons, beads or pom-poms.

8. A Living-Room Picnicfrom 18 mo From 18 mo · A blanket, a snack

Easier: One snack, one toy guest.

Harder: Plan the menu and set a place for every guest.

2-minute: Snack on a towel on the floor.

Safety: Soft food cut small, eaten sitting down. No whole grapes, nuts, popcorn or hard candy for little ones.

Coupon key · 9–16

Easier, harder, a 2-minute version and a safety line for every coupon

9. Paint Togetherfrom 2 yrs From 24 mo · Paper, washable paint, a brush

Easier: Water painting on a fence or a chalkboard.

Harder: Paint a picture of our day.

2-minute: Three brush strokes each.

Safety: Washable, non-food paint with a grown-up beside. An old shirt as a smock.

10. Bake Togetherfrom 3 yrs From 36 mo · A simple recipe

Easier: You press the shapes; the grown-up does the rest.

Harder: Read the recipe pictures and count the scoops.

2-minute: Squash one ball of dough together.

Safety: Only the grown-up uses the oven and hot trays. No tasting raw dough. Check allergies.

11. A Ball Gamefrom 1 yr From 12 mo · A soft ball

Easier: Sit facing each other and roll it back and forth.

Harder: Kick it between two shoes: a goal!

2-minute: Five rolls back and forth.

Safety: A soft ball too big to fit through a toilet-paper tube. Play away from roads and stairs.

12. A Box Carfrom 30 mo From 30 mo · A big cardboard box, crayons

Easier: Sit in the box; the grown-up pushes gently.

Harder: Add lights, a number plate and a map.

2-minute: Sit in the box and say “Beep beep!”

Safety: Take out staples and loose tape first. Push slowly on flat floors only.

13. A Treasure Huntfrom 30 mo From 30 mo · A toy to hide, paper

Easier: Hide it half showing, close by.

Harder: Three clues, one after another.

2-minute: Hide it under a cushion right beside you.

Safety: Hide only in safe, easy spots: never up high, near stairs or near cleaning things.

14. A Dress-Up Paradefrom 2 yrs From 24 mo · Hats, shirts, shoes

Easier: Just hats: on, off, on again.

Harder: Make a costume and tell us who you are.

2-minute: One hat each and one march around the room.

Safety: No scarves, ties, belts or cords. Take small parts off anything a child under 3 wears.

15. One Extra Storyfrom 1 yr From 12 mo · Any book

Easier: Look at the pictures and name what you see.

Harder: You tell the story from the pictures.

2-minute: One page, one cuddle.

Safety: A board book for little ones who still mouth pages.

16. You Choose the Playfrom 2 yrs From 24 mo · Whatever the play needs

Easier: Pick between two plays.

Harder: Plan the play and explain the rules.

2-minute: Pick a 2-minute play.

Safety: Grown-up checks the chosen play is safe for your child’s age before starting.

Safety and quick answers

Good to know

Every coupon follows these rules, and the coupon key has a safety line for each one. You know your child best: skip or change anything that doesn’t suit them.

A grown-up plays too

Every coupon is for a child and a grown-up together.

The toilet-paper tube test

For children under 3, anything that fits through a toilet-paper tube (about 1.25 in or 3.2 cm) stays out of reach.

Food and allergies

Soft food cut small, eaten sitting down. No whole grapes, nuts, popcorn or hard candy for little ones.

No cords, no balloons

No cords, ribbons, scarves or ties left with a child. No balloons for children under 8.

Quick answers

Which file do I print?

Pick one: US Letter or A4, color or low-ink. Every file has the same 11 pages. Print at “Actual size” or 100%.

Which pages?

Page 3 (fold card), 4 (reveal cards), 5–6 (coupons) and 7 (blank coupons and the cover). Pages 2 and 8–9 are for you.

What paper?

Card stock makes sturdy coupons; plain paper works. Print the fold card on its own sheet and leave the back blank for your message.

Can I type in it?

Yes, with a free PDF reader: the For and From lines, the reveal-card lines and the blank coupons. Pictures and colors can’t be changed.

What age is it for?

Ages 1–5. Every coupon shows a starting age in months. Pick the ones that fit; the coupon key has an easier version of each.

Can I share it?

Your license covers one household, plus the child you give the coupons to. Please don’t share the files.

These are everyday play ideas for families, not medical or developmental advice. Questions about your child’s growth or health? Your child’s doctor is a good place to start.
