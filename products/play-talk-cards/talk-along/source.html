Grown-up guide

Start here

Real talk happens in small moments: passing the peas, waiting at a red light, rinsing shampoo, turning off the lamp. These cards give those moments a good question.

How to use the cards

  1. Match the card to the moment. Dinner cards at the table, car cards on the road, bath cards at the tub, bedtime cards at lights-out.
  2. One card is plenty. A single good question can fill a whole meal. Put the rest back for tomorrow.
  3. Everyone answers. Grown-ups too. Kids open up when they hear your answer first.
  4. Use the grown-up tip. Each card has one small idea for keeping the talk going.
  5. Keep it light. Anyone can say “pass.” It’s a conversation, not a quiz.

Four moments

Dinner
Cards 01–13

Keep them in a jar on the table.

Car
Cards 14–26

Keep them in the glove box or a door pocket.

Bath
Cards 27–39

Keep them by the sink in a zip bag.

Bedtime
Cards 40–52

Keep them on the nightstand or by the books.

Ages 5–7. Read the card aloud, give an example answer, and welcome short answers. Drawing an answer counts too.

Ages 8–12. Let them read the card and choose who answers first. Ask the question back later in the week.

6 talk-along habits

Go first

Answer the card yourself sometimes. Kids copy what they see.

Ask “what else?”

One gentle follow-up keeps a short answer going.

Side by side

Car rides and dish duty feel easier than face-to-face talks.

Wait longer

Count to five in your head. Big thoughts take a moment.

Listen, don’t fix

Save advice for later. “Tell me more” goes a long way.

Passing is okay

Anyone can say “pass” and pull another card.

Why a question card? Kids often say more side by side than face to face. A card takes the pressure off: nobody is being quizzed, everyone gets a turn.
Three talk lines: “Tell me more.” · “What was that like?” · “I wonder…” Talk in the language you know best. Drawing, pointing or typing an answer counts too.
Most families love 2–3 of these and ask them again and again. Keep the favorites on top of the pile.

Some questions (the hard parts, the worries) can bring up big feelings. Listening is enough. If something your child shares worries you, reach out to your child’s doctor or another trusted professional.

Before you print

Print, cut and keep

  • Print at “Actual size” (100%). Cards are standard poker size, 2.5 × 3.5 in (63.5 × 88.9 mm).
  • Use cardstock if you can. Heavy paper or cardstock (65–110 lb / 176–300 gsm) feels like a real deck.
  • Edges cut off? Choose “Fit” instead. The cards print a little smaller and still work.
  • Backs are optional. Print the backs page on the reverse of a card sheet, flipping on the long edge. Test one sheet first.
  • Cut on the lines. A paper trimmer is fastest. Grown-up keeps the pieces and does the cutting. No time? Use the no-cut pages today.
  • Make them last. Laminate for sticky fingers. Keep them in a recipe box, a zip bag or on a ring.

9 cards per page · 6 pages · 54 cards
Prep: about 20 minutes to print and cut

Safety basics

  • In the car, a passenger reads the card. The driver keeps eyes on the road.
  • Bath time: stay with your child. Keep the cards dry in a zip bag by the sink.
  • Any question can be skipped. Nobody has to answer something that feels too big.
  • If a big worry comes up, listen first. Solutions can wait for a calm moment.
  • Grown-up keeps the pieces: cards, laminated cards and rings stay away from babies and toddlers.

Read a question straight from this page. The grown-up tip is in italics.

DinnerKeep them in a jar on the table.
  1. 01What was the best part of your day? What was the trickiest part? Go first with yours. Kids open up when grown-ups share too.
  2. 02If you could invent a brand-new food, what would be in it? What would you call it? Play along with silly answers. Silly keeps the talk going.
  3. 03Who made you smile today? What did they do? Listen for new names and ask one more question about them.
  4. 04If our family had a holiday that was just ours, what would we do on it? Write down the best idea, and try it for real.
  5. 05What’s something you’re getting better at? Notice the effort: “You kept practicing.”
  6. 06If you could have dinner with any animal, which one would you pick? What would you talk about? Ask one follow-up question before you share your own answer.
  7. 07What’s one thing you’d love to learn how to do? Ask what the very first step might be.
  8. 08If you were in charge of the house for a day, what rule would you make? Skip the correcting. Ask, “Why that rule?”
  9. 09What’s a question you’ve always wanted to ask me? Answer honestly and briefly, then ask it back.
  10. 10What made you laugh this week? Laugh along. Retelling a funny moment is storytelling practice.
  11. 11If you had a superpower just for helping people, what would it be? Ask who they would help first.
  12. 12Describe your perfect weekend, from breakfast to bedtime. Ask for details: “What would it smell like? Sound like?”
  13. 13What’s something kind that someone did for you lately? Say thanks for sharing. It doesn’t need to become a lesson.
CarA passenger reads; the driver just talks.
  1. 14Would you rather drive a bus, fly a plane or sail a boat? Why? Side-by-side talk feels easy. Eyes on the road is fine.
  2. 15Pick someone you can see outside. Make up a story about where they’re going. Take turns adding one sentence each.
  3. 16If this car could take us anywhere in one second, where would we go? Ask what they would pack.
  4. 17What song should be our family’s theme song? Let them pick the next song and ask why they like it.
  5. 18What’s something you’re looking forward to? Share something you’re looking forward to as well.
  6. 19If you designed a playground, what would it have? Ask them to describe it so clearly you could draw it.
  7. 20Pick a color. How many things that color can we spot before we get there? Count out loud together. Younger kids can point.
  8. 21What would you do if you were the grown-up and I were the kid today? Laugh along, then ask what they’d do the same as you.
  9. 22What’s the hardest part of being a kid right now? Just listen. “That sounds hard” is enough.
  10. 23If animals could talk, which one would be the funniest? What would it say? Do the voices together.
  11. 24Tell me about a dream you remember. Ask what they think it meant. There are no wrong answers.
  12. 25What’s something you know a lot about? Teach me one thing. Be the curious student. Ask a real question.
  13. 26Think of an animal. I’ll ask yes-or-no questions until I guess it. Swap roles so they practice asking the questions.

BathGrown-up stays close. Keep this page dry.
  1. 27If you could swim with any sea creature, which one would you choose? Ask what it would feel like to touch it.
  2. 28What would a fish say about our bathtub? Answer back in your best fish voice.
  3. 29What felt hard today? What helped? Warm water and a calm voice make this a gentle time to ask.
  4. 30If a bubble could carry a message anywhere, what would yours say? Blow the message away together.
  5. 31What’s your favorite smell in the whole world? Why? Share yours. Smells often bring back stories.
  6. 32If you were a raindrop, where would you want to land? Ask, “And then what happens?”
  7. 33Invent a new bath toy. What does it do? Ask them to give it a name and a sound.
  8. 34What makes someone a good friend? Ask for an example from their day.
  9. 35Would you rather live under the sea or up in the clouds? Ask about the trickiest part of living there.
  10. 36You just found a new planet. What’s its name, and who lives there? Build the world together, one detail each.
  11. 37What’s something you like about yourself? Leave a pause. Then share something you like about them.
  12. 38Close your eyes. How many sounds can you hear right now? Listen quietly first, then compare lists.
  13. 39If you shrank to the size of a rubber duck, what would you explore? Follow their lead and keep asking, “Then what?”
BedtimeKeep them on the nightstand or by the books.
  1. 40What are three good things from today? Keep it small. A snack, a joke or a sunny minute all count.
  2. 41What’s something you’re wondering about? “I don’t know. Let’s find out tomorrow” is a great answer.
  3. 42If you could choose tonight’s dream, what would it be? Speak slowly and softly. Calm voices help everyone wind down.
  4. 43Who is someone you’re thankful for? Why? Ask if they’d like to tell that person tomorrow.
  5. 44What was a brave thing you did this week? Name the feeling: “You felt nervous, and you did it anyway.”
  6. 45What’s your favorite memory of our family? Share one of your favorite memories of them.
  7. 46If your stuffed animal could talk, what would it say about your day? Let the toy answer in its own voice.
  8. 47What’s something you’d like to do together this weekend? Pick one idea and put it on the calendar.
  9. 48Was there anything that worried you today? Listen first. Solutions can wait until morning.
  10. 49Start a story: “Once upon a time, a tiny dragon…” Take turns adding one line until someone yawns.
  11. 50What would you love to be really good at when you grow up? Ask what they love about it.
  12. 51If today were weather, what would it be? Sunny, cloudy, stormy? Accept every answer. Feelings aren’t right or wrong.
  13. 52What do you want to remember about today? End with the same goodnight words every night.
