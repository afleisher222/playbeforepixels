Grown-up guide · 1 of 2

How the countdown works

Winter days can be long and dark. This countdown gives each one a small, warm play to look forward to. It takes about two minutes to start, and there is no wrong way to do it.

1

Print

Print pages 6–22 (or the whole file). Card stock is nice but plain paper works.

2

Pick a way

Cut the 12 card pages in half and tuck each card in an envelope, bag or jar with its number tag. Or skip the cutting and use the board.

3

Pick a start day

Any day works. Many families start on the first of the month; others start the first snowy week or the first day of a break.

Three ways to use it

Card a day

Open one numbered card each morning. Play it when it suits your day, even at bedtime.

Board only

Hang the countdown board. Read the day’s play from the list page. Your child marks the square after you play.

Pick and choose

Skip a day, swap two days, play a favorite twice. The numbers are there to help, not to boss you around.

Most children love 2–3 of these and want them again and again. That’s the countdown working. Play the favorites as often as you like, and let the rest wait for another winter.

Why a play a day?

Young children learn words and ideas in back-and-forth moments with the people who love them: a look, a laugh, a turn, a word. A short play each day adds more of those moments to ordinary winter days. The screens in your home keep their usual spot; this is simply something warm to add.

Grown-up guide · 2 of 2

Talk while you play

Every card has one talk line. You don’t need to say it word for word. These three moves do most of the work:

Pause and wait

Say a little, then stop and count to five in your head. A look, a sound or a wiggle is an answer.

“Ready, set… (wait)”

Say what you see

Put words to what your child is doing, right as it happens. No quiz questions needed.

“Drip, drip. It’s melting!”

Offer a choice

Hold up two things and wait. Pointing and reaching count as answers.

“Big cup or little cup?”

Talk, sing and read in the language you know best. Every talk line works in any language. A sign, a point or a tap on a talking device counts as communicating, just like a word.

Before you play

Our safety rules

Every play in this countdown follows these rules, and each card has its own safety line. You know your child best: skip or change anything that doesn’t suit them.

A grown-up plays too

Every play is for a child and a grown-up together. Stay close and watch.

The toilet-paper tube test

For children under 3, anything that fits through a toilet-paper tube (about 1.25 in or 3.2 cm across) stays out of reach. When in doubt, leave it out.

Water and ice

Stay within arm’s reach during any water play, even a tray of melting ice. Warm water only: test it on your wrist.

No cords, no balloons

No cords, strings, scarves or ties long enough to go around a neck. No balloons for children under 8.

Food and allergies

Soft food cut small and thin, eaten sitting down. No whole grapes, nuts, popcorn, marshmallows or hard candy for little ones. Check allergies first.

Lights, batteries and heat

Use a flashlight with a screwed-shut battery door and keep button batteries away from children. Keep hot drinks and the stove out of every play.

Windows and climbing

Look through closed windows from the floor. A grown-up lifts; nobody stands on sills or furniture.

Cold days outside

Dress for the weather and come in when little hands and noses feel cold. Hold hands near roads.

These are everyday play ideas for families, not medical or developmental advice. Questions about your child’s growth or health? Your child’s doctor is a good place to start.

Day1

Paper Snowball Toss

from 2 yrs With a grown-up
From 24 mo No prep A little mess About 10 min Nothing to buy

You need: Scrap paper, a laundry basket

Crumple sheets of paper into big snowballs. Take turns tossing them into the basket. After each round, everyone takes one giant step back.

Talk while you play · Pause and wait

“Ready, set… (wait) throw!”

Make it easier: Stand right by the basket and drop them in.

Make it harder: Count the snowballs that land in. Try the other hand.

Tired? 2-minute version: Three snowballs each, one throw each, then tidy up together.

Safety: Crumple whole sheets into big balls. Pick up small torn bits so they stay out of mouths.

Mitten Match

from 2 yrs With a grown-up
From 24 mo Prep 2 min No mess About 5 min Nothing to buy

You need: Everyone’s mittens, gloves and socks

Tip everyone’s mittens, gloves and socks into one pile. Find the pairs together. Hold one up and ask, “Where’s its friend?”

“Same! … Not the same.”

Make it harder: Sort by owner too: “Whose mittens are these?”

Tired? 2-minute version: Match two pairs, then wave hello with mitten hands.

Safety: Take off any cords or clips that join mittens before you play.

Day5

Ice Rescue

from 2 yrs With a grown-up
From 30 mo Prep 2 min (the night before) Messy About 15 min Nothing to buy

You need: A plastic tub, water, 3 big bath toys, a baking tray, spoons

The night before, freeze big bath toys in a tub of water. Tip the ice block onto a baking tray. Pour warm water and scrape with spoons until every toy is free.

Talk while you play · Say what you see

“Drip, drip… it’s melting! Who’s coming out?”

Make it easier: Freeze a thin layer so the toys pop out fast.

Make it harder: Guess which toy will come out first. Were you right?

Tired? 2-minute version: Touch the ice block, then a warm cup: “Cold! Warm!”

Safety: Toys too big to fit through a toilet-paper tube. Test warm water on your wrist. Stay within arm’s reach.

From 24 mo No prep No mess About 5 min Nothing to buy

You need: Two socks rolled into a ball

Printing and quick answers

Good to know

Which file do I print?

Pick one: US Letter or A4, color or low-ink. Every file has the same 25 pages. Print at “Actual size” or 100%, not “Fit”.

Which pages?

Pages 6–8 (boards and list), 9–20 (play cards), 21–22 (number tags) and 23 (certificate). The guide pages are for you to read on screen.

What paper?

Plain paper is fine. Card stock makes cards and tags last longer. Laminating is optional.

We started late. Is that okay?

Yes. The plays aren’t tied to dates. Start on any day and play as many days as you like.

Is this tied to a holiday?

No. It’s a winter countdown for any family: snow, cold, cozy days and long nights. Use it to count down to a holiday, a break, a birthday or nothing at all.

We have a baby and a preschooler.

Every card says its starting age. Let the baby watch from a lap and keep small things out of reach. Many plays have an easier version for younger children.

Can I type in it?

Yes, in the color and low-ink files, with a free PDF reader: the blank board squares, the Winter Walk Hunt tick boxes and the certificate lines. Pictures and colors can’t be changed.

Can I share it?

Your license covers one household, including grandparents and sitters who care for your child. Please don’t share the files. A gift passes the license to the family who receives it.
