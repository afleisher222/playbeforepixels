Section A · Grown-up guide · 1 of 2For grown-upsSetup 2 min

Start here, grown-ups

This kit gives your day a simple shape: jobs first, then play and time together, then screens at their usual spot. Nothing to take away, only more play to add.

1 · Jobs first
2 · Then play and time together
3 · Then screens, at their spot
Set up in 2 minutes
1
Pick one checklist per child. Pictures for ages 2–5, words for ages 5–12, or the fillable blank. Choose a colorway they like.
2
Write in your screen spot. For example, “after dinner”. Keep it the same every day, and the same length.
3
Cut the together tokens. Keep them in an envelope. A grown-up keeps the pieces.
4
Put it at eye level. Walk through it together once: “First jobs, then we play, then screens.”
Why play first, in plain words

Children practice talking, taking turns and solving problems through back-and-forth: you say something, they answer, you answer back. Jobs, play and meals are full of these turns. Putting them first simply makes more room for them in the day. For ages 1–4, the World Health Organization suggests at least 180 minutes of active play a day (WHO, 2019).

Three talk lines for any page
Pause and wait.
Count to five in your head. Give them a turn to talk, point or sign.
Say what you see.
“You put out the spoons. One, two, three spoons!”
Repeat and add one word.
“Ball.” “Big ball!” “Big red ball!”

Talk, sing and read in the language you know best. Every language counts. A sign, a point or a tap counts as communicating, too. Write your own words, in any language, on the blank pages.

Section A · Grown-up guide · 2 of 2For grown-ups

How it works at each age

Use what fits: ages are a starting point, and pictures work at any age. Most children love 2 or 3 of these tools; that’s normal.

Ages 2–5

Point and say each picture on the checklist. Use the Play-First Board to show first, then, later. Two or three helping jobs a day is plenty.

Ages 5–8

They tick their own boxes on the ages 5–12 checklist and pick the together token. Add 3 or 4 jobs from the family jobs chart.

Ages 8–12

Fill in the blank checklist together and let them write their own jobs and play. Make the Family Plan together; let them lead one part.

How screens fit in this kit
  • Screens have a fixed spot in the day, the same time and about the same length each day.
  • The spot never grows or shrinks with jobs or behavior. That keeps screens from becoming the prize, so jobs and play can simply be part of the day.
  • Tokens are for play and time together (a story, a game, a walk), never screen minutes. Time together is never taken away as a punishment.
  • A talker (a device a child uses to talk) is their voice, not screen time. It stays with them at meals, in the car and at night.
  • End gently with the screen-spot cards: 5 more minutes, then Screens go to sleep, then What we do next.
5 more minutes
Screens go to sleep
What we do next
When stopping is hard

Stay close and name it: “It’s hard to stop. You wish it was longer.” Then show What we do next. Big feelings at the end are normal, and the spot comes back tomorrow.

If interest fades

Move the chart, swap in a fresh token, or fill in a blank checklist for a grown-up; kids love ticking yours. Tired day? Just say the rhythm out loud: “Jobs, play, then screens.” That counts.

Section A · Printing, laminating, velcro and safetyFor grown-upsPrep 20 min total

Print it, make it last

About 20 minutes to print and cut, then reusable. The checklists need no cutting: start today.

Printing
  • Print at 100% / Actual size. Don’t use “fit to page” for tokens and cards.
  • US Letter file for the US and Canada; A4 for everywhere else.
  • Charts: regular paper. Tokens, cards and the board: cardstock (65–110 lb / 176–300 gsm).
  • The low-ink file has white backgrounds and line art children can color.
Make it last
  • Laminate the charts and use a dry-erase marker, week after week.
  • No laminator? Slide charts into clear page protectors. Dry-erase works on those too.
  • Laminate tokens before cutting, then cut on the dashed lines, leaving a thin sealed edge.
Velcro: ages 3 and up only
  • Velcro dots are small enough to be a choking risk for under-3s.
  • Under 3: no dots. Lay tokens on top of the board, or slip the board into a page protector and tuck the token inside.
  • Check dots before each play; remove any that lift.
Shopping list (whole kit, printed once)
  • Regular paper: about 10 sheets (the charts you pick)
  • Cardstock: 4 sheets (2 token sheets, cards, board)
  • 6 laminating pouches, or 6 page protectors
  • 1 dry-erase marker · 1 envelope for tokens
  • Optional, ages 3+: 12 pairs of velcro dots
Safety notes
  • Grown-up keeps the pieces. Every cut piece is 2.1 in (5.3 cm) or larger, bigger than the toilet-paper-tube test for under-3s. Print at 100% so they stay that size, and store them out of reach.
  • A grown-up stays close for every play. Water, bubbles and cooking: always within arm’s reach. Grown-ups handle knives, heat and glass.
  • Every play follows our published safety rules.

Check your print

After printing the tokens, measure this square. It should be 2.1 in (5.3 cm) on each side. If it’s smaller, your printer shrank the page: print again at 100% / Actual size.

Where and when screens rest

Pick a few screen-free times and places. Fewer rules, kept kindly, work better than many.

Screens rest…

A talker (a device a child uses to talk) never rests: it’s their voice, so it stays with them.

At night, our screens sleep in

A charging spot outside bedrooms makes bedtime easier for everyone, grown-ups included.

Watching and playing together

When you can, choose together, watch together, and talk about it after: “Who was your favorite? What would you do?”

Shows, games or videos we enjoy together:
What the guidelines say
  • Ages 1–4: at least 180 minutes a day of active play, in any mix. No screen time under age 1; no more than 1 hour a day at ages 2–4 (WHO, 2019).
  • Ages 2–5: about 1 hour a day of high-quality programs, watched together when you can (AAP, 2016).
  • For families: choose screen-free times and places together, like meals, car rides and bedrooms, and make a family plan (AAP, 2016). For older children, there’s no single number; this plan helps you choose what fits.

Sources: World Health Organization, Guidelines on physical activity, sedentary behaviour and sleep for children under 5 years of age (2019). American Academy of Pediatrics, “Media and Young Minds,” Pediatrics 2016;138(5). Every family is different; your pediatrician is a good person to ask about your child.
