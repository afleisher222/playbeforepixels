## In plain language

This review looked at the setting of screen use in children under 6: what type, what content, whether a caregiver joined in, and why screens were used. More program viewing and background TV went with poorer thinking and learning outcomes. More program viewing, content meant for older ages and caregivers' own screen use went with poorer social and emotional outcomes. Using screens together with a caregiver went with better thinking and learning outcomes. Effects were small to moderate.

## Who was studied

Studies of children aged 0 to 5.99 years; 7,441 records screened. Number of studies [VERIFY].

## What was found

- Context matters: background TV and solo viewing linked with poorer outcomes; co-use with better ones.

## Limitations

- Observational inputs.
- Varied, mostly parent-reported measures.
- Not autism-specific.

## What it does NOT show

- Anything about autism.
- That changing context changes outcomes.
