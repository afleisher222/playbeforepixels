## In plain language

These are Romanian-language write-ups of the same idea and, as far as we can tell, the same group of children as Zamfir's 2018 paper. Secondary summaries say about 80% of the children were boys, and that hyperactivity and lack of communication were the first things parents noticed. The author also describes individual children who changed visibly within two to three weeks after screens were stopped.

## Who was studied

The same Romanian clinical population as the 2018 paper [VERIFY].

## What was found

- Reports that about 80% of identified children were boys [VERIFY].
- Describes individual children with visible change within 2 to 3 weeks after screen use stopped. These are anecdotes with no comparison group.

## Limitations

- Not peer reviewed.
- Same author and same data as the 2018 paper, so it is not independent evidence.
- Stories about quick change have no comparison group. Young children change fast for many reasons, including ordinary development and extra adult attention.
- None of these texts could be opened. Details come only from search summaries.

## What it does NOT show

- That stopping screens changes a child's developmental path.
- That the children did not have autism.
