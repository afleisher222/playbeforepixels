## In plain language

French child psychiatrist Bruno Harlé argued that intensive early screen exposure, more than about four hours a day, might play a part in autism-like signs in some possibly vulnerable children under 6. He proposed the label "virtual autism". The paper brings together published case descriptions and clinical reports and makes an argument from them. It does not collect new data. It carried the term from Zamfir's paper into an indexed neuroscience and education journal.

## Who was studied

No new participants. According to a later review [VERIFY], the paper compiles case descriptions from France, Japan, Qatar, Romania, the USA, Thailand and Tunisia. The children described were typically under 4 and exposed to screens from their first year.

## What was found

- Argues that there is "growing clinical and empirical evidence" for a causal role of intensive early screen exposure in some younger children (wording from an abstract snippet [VERIFY]).
- Proposes the name "virtual autism".

## Limitations

- The paper makes the case for a hypothesis. It does not test one.
- Its evidence is case reports and uncontrolled clinical series, which cannot establish causation.
- It does not rule out reverse causation (early autistic traits leading to more screen use) or confounding (other family factors behind both). Later genetic and statistical studies made those alternatives concrete: see Takahashi N 2023 and Lin 2025.

## What it does NOT show

- That screens produce autism.
- How often any such pattern happens.
- Anything about whether autism-like signs go away when screens are reduced.
