## In plain language

This August 2026 review says "virtual autism" and autism share observable features, but that the concept's proponents describe different proposed origins and a different course. It says the concept is not a diagnosis. It names critical gaps: no long-term studies following screen-associated signs over time, and no randomized trials of screen reduction. It recommends limiting and delaying early screen use and encouraging face-to-face play.

## Who was studied

No new data.

## What was found

- The concept is not a diagnosis.
- There are no longitudinal studies of the course of screen-associated signs and no randomized trials of screen reduction.

## Limitations

- A narrative review.
- Some summaries restate the concept's claims (for example "potentially reversible", or "unlike ASD, which has genetic origins") as fact. Those claims are not established, and we do not repeat them as fact.
- Whether it engages with the null, bias-corrected and genetic evidence is not confirmed.

## What it does NOT show

- That screens produce autism-like signs, or that such signs go away with less screen time.
