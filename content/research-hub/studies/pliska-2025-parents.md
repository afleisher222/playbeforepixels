## In plain language

Researchers surveyed 117 parents of autistic children and 58 parents of non-autistic children about media. Parents of autistic children were more concerned about media effects on health and behavior. They also considered more daily media time appropriate (about 151 vs. 93 minutes). The paper's introduction describes "virtual autism" as not an official diagnosis and as something to distinguish from autism.

## Who was studied

175 parents in an online survey, probably in Germany [VERIFY].

## What was found

- More concern about media effects among parents of autistic children.
- More daily media time judged appropriate by those parents (150.76 vs. 93.05 minutes).

## Limitations

- A survey of attitudes. It does not measure child outcomes.

## What it does NOT show

- Anything about whether screens affect autism or development.
