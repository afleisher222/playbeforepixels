## In plain language

Japanese researchers built genetic scores for autism and ADHD for 437 children, using large international genetic studies. Parents reported screen time at 24, 32 and 40 months. Children with a higher genetic likelihood of autism already spent more time on screens from early childhood. A higher genetic likelihood of ADHD went with screen time rising over time. The authors suggest that long screen time may be an early sign of a developing neurodevelopmental condition rather than its cause.

## Who was studied

437 children of Japanese descent in the Hamamatsu Birth Cohort for Mothers and Children.

## What was found

- Higher autism genetic scores went with more screen time from early childhood. Press summaries report about 1.5 times the likelihood of being in a roughly 3-hours-a-day group and 2.1 times for 4 hours or more [VERIFY].
- Higher ADHD genetic scores went with screen time rising over time.

## Limitations

- Small sample, one ancestry group.
- Genetic scores explain only a small share of genetic likelihood.
- Parent-reported screen time.
- Effect sizes come from press summaries.

## What it does NOT show

- That screens have no effect. Genes predicting screen use does not rule out an added effect of screens.
