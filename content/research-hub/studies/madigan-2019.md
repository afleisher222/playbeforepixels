## In plain language

This Canadian study followed mothers and children in Calgary [VERIFY] and asked whether screen time at ages 2 and 3 went with scores on a developmental screening questionnaire (the ASQ-3) later on. Reviewers recall that more screen time was associated with slightly lower later scores, and that lower scores did not predict later screen time [VERIFY]. We will not quote any figure until the abstract is read.

## Who was studied

About 2,400 mother-child pairs in the All Our Families cohort, Calgary, Canada [VERIFY].

## What was found

- Association between more screen time and lower later screening scores [VERIFY direction and size].

## Limitations

- Observational; parent-reported screen time with no content or co-viewing data.
- The ASQ-3 is a screener, not a diagnosis.
- Effects are recalled as small; other family factors may play a part.

## What it does NOT show

- Anything about autism.
- That screen time produced lower scores.
