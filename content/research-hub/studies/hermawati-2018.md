## In plain language

Doctors in Semarang, Indonesia, described a very small group of young children who had heavy early screen exposure and autism-like signs. All had speech delay and a short attention span. Children who watched 3 or more hours a day also showed hyperactivity. In two-thirds of cases there was no parent-child interaction during screen time. The authors suggested a possible biological mechanism, without testing it.

## Who was studied

A small series of young children at a university hospital in Semarang, Indonesia. The exact number is not confirmed; the percentages reported suggest three children [VERIFY].

## What was found

- Speech delay and a short attention span were reported in all cases.
- Hyperactivity was reported in 66.6%, in children watching 3 or more hours a day.
- 66.6% had no parent-child interaction during screen use.

## Limitations

- A very small, uncontrolled case series.
- Whether standardized autism instruments were used is not confirmed.
- The proposed mechanism (involving light-sensitive retinal cells and a brain chemical called GABA) is speculative.

## What it does NOT show

- That screens were responsible for the children's difficulties.
- Anything about how common this is.
