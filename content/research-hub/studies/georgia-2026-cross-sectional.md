## In plain language

A survey of caregivers of 1,834 children aged 16 to 30 months in Georgia, collected in clinics and online from April 2025 to January 2026. Average daily screen time was 64 minutes at assessment and higher when averaged over the past six months or year. About half the children had been exposed before 12 months. Higher average daily screen time went with higher autism-screening scores.

## Who was studied

1,834 children aged 16 to 30 months; 62.8% boys; 74.6% urban. Screening risk: 77.6% low, 17.6% medium, 4.9% high.

## What was found

- Mean daily screen time: 64.1 minutes at assessment, 93.8 over the past six months, 108.4 over the past year.
- 12.2% exposed before 6 months; 49.8% before 12 months.
- Higher average screen time was associated with higher M-CHAT-R/F scores (effect size [VERIFY]).

## Limitations

- Cross-sectional.
- Online recruitment invites selection bias.
- Screening scores, not diagnoses; recalled exposure.
- May overlap with the research group behind the 2025 Tbilisi study [VERIFY].

## What it does NOT show

- The direction of the association.
