## In plain language

Researchers in Singapore looked at 5,336 toddlers aged 17 to 24 months at routine primary-care visits. Average daily screen time was 1.31 hours. Toddlers above that average had about 24% higher odds of a positive result on the M-CHAT-R/F autism screen. The link was strongest for social-communication items.

## Who was studied

5,336 typically developing children aged 17 to 24 months, multi-ethnic, Singapore.

## What was found

- Average screen time 1.31 hours a day.
- Above-average screen time: odds ratio about 1.24 for a positive screen [VERIFY; the figure came from news coverage].
- Strongest for social-communication items.

## Limitations

- Cross-sectional: it cannot tell whether screen time came first or whether early social-communication differences led to more screen use.
- A positive screen is not a diagnosis.
- Parent report. Adjustment and confidence interval [VERIFY].

## What it does NOT show

- That screen time raises the chance of autism.
