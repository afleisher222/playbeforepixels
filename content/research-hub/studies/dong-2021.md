## In plain language

Researchers studied 193 autistic preschoolers in China. They averaged 2.64 hours a day of screen time; 48% used two or more device types; only 34% had screen time with a parent who talked with them. More screen time correlated weakly with lower developmental scores, poorer parent-child relationship scores and higher autism severity. Lower paternal education, fewer limits set by caregivers and more caregiver screen time went with more child screen time.

## Who was studied

193 autistic preschool children, China.

## What was found

- Average 2.64 ± 2.24 hours a day.
- Weak correlations: developmental quotient r = -0.234; parent-child relationship r = -0.180; autism severity r = 0.192.
- Family factors associated with more screen time: lower paternal education, fewer limits, more caregiver screen use.

## Limitations

- Only autistic children, so it says nothing about onset.
- Cross-sectional.
- Weak correlations.

## What it does NOT show

- That screens contribute to autism.
