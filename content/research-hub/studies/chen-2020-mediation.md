## In plain language

Using the same Shenzhen survey, the researchers asked whether less caregiver-child interaction, shorter sleep or less outdoor activity statistically account for part of the link between early screen exposure and autistic-like behaviors. One summary reports that caregiver-child interaction accounted for about 5.3% of the association and sleep about 1.2%, while outdoor activity did not account for any [VERIFY; another summary did not show these figures].

## Who was studied

The same 2017 Longhua survey (29,461 pairs, per one summary [VERIFY]).

## What was found

- Small shares of the association statistically accounted for by caregiver interaction and sleep [VERIFY].

## Limitations

- Cross-sectional data. "Mediation" here is a statistical description, not proof of a pathway.
- Same data and limits as Chen 2021.

## What it does NOT show

- How screens, talk and sleep actually relate in a child's development.
