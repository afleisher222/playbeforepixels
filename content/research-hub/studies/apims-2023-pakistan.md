## In plain language

Clinicians in Islamabad followed 30 children under 10 (average age about 4) who had speech delay, more than 5 hours a day of screens, and an autism classification on the ADOS assessment. After 10 to 12 months of professional speech and behavior sessions, with screen time cut to 30 minutes a day or less, repeat ADOS testing placed 24 of 30 children outside the autism range, 3 borderline and 3 in the mild range. The authors concluded that heavy screen time "can lead to" autism-like signs that are reversible with early intervention. Their design cannot support that conclusion.

## Who was studied

30 children seen at two outpatient clinics in Islamabad, January 2020 to December 2022.

## What was found

- Repeat ADOS: 24 of 30 outside the autism range, 3 borderline, 3 mild.

## Limitations

- No control group, so growing up, the professional sessions and screen reduction cannot be told apart.
- Re-assessment was not blinded.
- Small sample; authors not confirmed.
- The authors' causal conclusion does not follow from an uncontrolled design.
- The outcome was where each child fell on an autism assessment. It did not measure the children's well-being, comfort or everyday communication, and a score outside "the autism range" is not the same as a child being better off.
- "Reversible" is the authors' word. Autism is a lifelong difference, and this design cannot show that any child stopped being autistic.

## What it does NOT show

- That screens produced the children's difficulties.
- That reducing screens is why scores changed.
