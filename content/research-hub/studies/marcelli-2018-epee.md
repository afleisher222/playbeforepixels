## In plain language

Three French clinicians proposed the name EPEE (early and excessive screen exposure) for a set of signs they said they saw in young children with heavy screen exposure: attention problems, language delay, fine-motor difficulties, an increasingly exclusive interest in screens, and relationship difficulties such as aggression or restlessness. They said it can appear by 8 to 10 months and that it lessens, or even disappears, when exposure stops quickly. The name deliberately avoids the word "autism".

## Who was studied

Clinical observations of children from about 5 to 6 months up to 4 to 5 years old in France. No systematic sample.

## What was found

- Describes a cluster of signs in heavily exposed young children and proposes a name for it.
- Claims the signs lessen when exposure stops. The paper does not test this.

## Limitations

- Clinical impressions, with no systematic data, no comparison group and no standardized measures.
- EPEE is not recognized in the WHO's ICD or in the DSM.
- In 2017 one co-author posted a widely shared video warning about screens and autism. French scientists and autism commentators criticized it widely [VERIFY].

## What it does NOT show

- That EPEE is a distinct condition.
- That the signs described lessen because screens were reduced, rather than with time or other changes.
