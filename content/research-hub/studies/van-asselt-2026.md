## In plain language

An autistic author argues that discussion of screens in autism is dominated by "risk-oriented, deficit-based, and alarmist narratives" built around "screen time". The author describes how screen use met their own needs for predictability, safety, autonomy and belonging. The piece proposes six principles: terms that capture how and why media are used; attention to the functions and settings of use; weighing benefits as well as risks; outcomes centred on autistic autonomy and well-being; respect for neurodivergent developmental paths; and participatory, mixed-methods research.

## Who was studied

Not a study. Lived experience plus a narrative review.

## What was found

- Six principles for more respectful, more useful research on screens and autism.

## Limitations

- A perspective with no new data.
- Focuses on autistic people's own screen use, not on infant exposure.

## What it does NOT show

- It is a perspective, so it neither shows nor rules out any effect of screens.
