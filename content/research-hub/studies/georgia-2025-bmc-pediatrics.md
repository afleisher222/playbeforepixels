## In plain language

Researchers at three paediatric clinics in Tbilisi, Georgia, studied 646 children aged 16 to 30 months in 2024. High-risk autism screening scores were about three times as common among children first exposed to screens before 6 months (12.24% vs. 4.33%). Average daily screen time was only weakly related to the score, explaining about 2.3% of the variation. The authors suggest that timing may matter more than duration, and call for prospective studies that account for other factors.

## Who was studied

646 children (377 boys, 269 girls) aged 16 to 30 months, January to August 2024.

## What was found

- High-risk scores: 12.24% with first exposure before 6 months vs. 4.33% otherwise.
- Daily screen time explained about 2.3% of the variation in scores.

## Limitations

- A clinic sample, with exposure recalled when the child was screened.
- Limited adjustment for other factors.
- Screening scores, not diagnoses.
- Parents of babies with early differences might introduce screens earlier (reverse causation).

## What it does NOT show

- That early screens contribute to autism.
