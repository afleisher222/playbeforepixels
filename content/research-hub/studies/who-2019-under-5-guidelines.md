## In plain language

The WHO's 2019 guideline for children under 5 covers physical activity, sitting still and sleep. On screens it says: no screen time for infants under 1, and no more than 1 hour a day for children aged 2 to 4. It recommends at least 180 minutes a day of varied physical activity for children aged 1 to 4. Reviewers recall that it also does not recommend sedentary screen time for 1-year-olds, that it says "less is better" for 2-to-4-year-olds, and that it encourages reading and storytelling with a caregiver during quiet time [VERIFY all three against the PDF].

## Who was studied

Guidance for infants under 1, children aged 1 to 2, and children aged 3 to 4.

## What was found

- No screen time under age 1.
- No more than 1 hour a day for ages 2 to 4.
- 180 minutes of varied physical activity a day for ages 1 to 4.

## Limitations

- Guidance, not a study.
- The screen limits are precautionary and rest on low-certainty observational evidence [VERIFY].

## What it does NOT show

- Anything about autism.
- That following the limits changes any individual child's development.
