## In plain language

Mendelian randomization uses genetic variants linked to a behavior as a natural experiment. These researchers used variants linked to leisure screen time in large, mostly adult, European-ancestry genetic studies. They found no evidence that genetically predicted leisure screen time affects autism. It was associated with lower ADHD odds and higher intellectual-disability odds, results that suggest the genetic proxies also capture other traits.

## Who was studied

Genetic summary data from 703,901 participants in 51 studies (256,725 in the screen-time sample), European ancestry.

## What was found

- Autism: no causal signal.
- ADHD: odds ratio 0.68 (95% CI 0.52-0.89) per standard deviation of genetically predicted leisure screen time.
- Intellectual disability: odds ratio 1.66 (1.26-2.18).

## Limitations

- The genetic proxies reflect a tendency toward leisure screen time, mostly in adults. They do not measure what a caregiver chooses for an infant or toddler.
- Such proxies are probably tied to other traits, such as education (pleiotropy). The unexpected ADHD and intellectual-disability results suggest this.
- European ancestry only.

## What it does NOT show

- Anything direct about screen exposure in the first years of life.
