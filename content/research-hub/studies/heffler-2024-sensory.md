## In plain language

Using the same US National Children's Study data as Heffler 2020, researchers linked TV and video exposure at 12, 18 and 24 months with parent-reported sensory processing at about 33 months [VERIFY age]. After adjusting for birth and family factors, early exposure was associated with atypical sensory processing in several areas, such as seeking or avoiding sensations. Results differed by age at exposure.

## Who was studied

1,471 US children.

## What was found

- Early TV/video exposure associated with atypical sensory-processing scores (effect sizes [VERIFY]).

## Limitations

- A parent-report sensory profile, not an autism assessment.
- Observational.
- Same research group and dataset as Heffler 2020.

## What it does NOT show

- Anything about autism. Sensory differences are common in autism but are not autism.
