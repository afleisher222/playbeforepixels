## In plain language

Researchers in Tunisia compared 150 autistic children aged 1 to 4 with 150 non-autistic children. Autistic children had started TV earlier (about 4 vs. 14 months) and watched far longer (about 7 hours a day vs. under 1). They mostly watched alone, preferred repetitive content such as music videos, and more often looked at the screen out of the corner of the eye (36.7% vs. 0%) or blinked repetitively (20.4% vs. 4.7%). Parents often used TV to occupy or soothe the child. The authors associate this exposure with more autistic traits.

## Who was studied

150 autistic and 150 non-autistic children aged 1 to 4 in Tunisia.

## What was found

- Earlier start (about 4 vs. 14 months) and much longer daily viewing (about 7 hours vs. under 1).
- Mostly solitary viewing with a preference for repetitive content.
- Distinctive viewing behaviors, such as side-on gaze, were more common among autistic children.

## Limitations

- Children were already diagnosed, and exposure was recalled afterwards.
- Distinctive viewing behaviors and a preference for repetitive content fit with autism that was already present. The data fit reverse causation at least as well as any effect of screens.
- Authors not confirmed.

## What it does NOT show

- That TV contributed to autism.
