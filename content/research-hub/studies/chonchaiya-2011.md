## In plain language

Thai researchers compared 54 autistic children (average age about 2.6) with 84 non-autistic children (about 2.4). Parents reported that the autistic children had watched about three times as much television and had started about six months earlier. Thailand is one of the countries in Harlé's case compilation.

## Who was studied

54 autistic children and 84 controls in Thailand.

## What was found

- About three times as much TV among autistic children.
- TV viewing began about six months earlier.

## Limitations

- Parents recalled viewing after the diagnosis, so memory may be biased.
- Small, clinic-based sample.
- More and earlier TV among children later diagnosed can reflect early autistic traits or how parents coped, as well as any effect of TV.
- One summary said autistic children watched "more often with their parents", but that may paraphrase other work. We do not use it until the abstract is read [VERIFY].

## What it does NOT show

- Which came first, or that TV contributed to autism.
