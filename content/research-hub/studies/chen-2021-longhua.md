## In plain language

Researchers surveyed caregivers of 29,461 kindergarten children in Longhua District, Shenzhen, in 2017. Parents recalled their child's screen use from birth to 3 and completed a checklist of autistic-like behaviors. Earlier first exposure, longer daily screen time and more years of exposure were each associated with autistic-like behaviors.

## Who was studied

29,461 child-caregiver pairs at kindergartens in Shenzhen, China.

## What was found

- Earlier start, more daily time and more cumulative years of screens each went with autistic-like behaviors (effect sizes [VERIFY]).

## Limitations

- Cross-sectional, with infant screen use recalled years later. Recall may be affected by parents' worries about development.
- A parent-rated checklist, not a diagnosis.
- Cannot address direction or other family factors.
- Probably shares its data with Chen 2020, so the two are not independent evidence.

## What it does NOT show

- That screens contributed to the behaviors.
