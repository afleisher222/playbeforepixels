## In plain language

Researchers at the UC Davis MIND Institute studied 120 three-year-olds, some with an older sibling who is autistic or has ADHD. Children with ADHD concerns watched more screen media than comparison children. Across all groups, more screen time went with lower language scores. Screen time was related to the child's own behavior, not to family history. The summaries did not report a difference for the autism group, but that is not confirmed.

## Who was studied

120 children aged 36 months: autism (n=20), ADHD concerns (n=14) and comparison (n=86).

## What was found

- More screen time in the ADHD-concerns group than the comparison group.
- More screen time went with lower receptive and expressive language across groups.
- Screen time related to the child's behavior, not family history.

## Limitations

- Small groups.
- Cross-sectional at one age.
- Parent report.
- The autism-group result needs confirming before use.

## What it does NOT show

- The direction of any effect.
