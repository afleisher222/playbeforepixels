## In plain language

Researchers in India compared 43 autistic children under 5 with 43 age-matched non-autistic children, asking about screen time, age at first exposure, device, content and viewing context. The unadjusted odds of autism were higher with 2 to 4 hours a day. After adjustment, the odds ratios became very large, with confidence intervals so wide that they carry almost no information.

## Who was studied

86 hospital-based children under 5 in India.

## What was found

- 2 to 4 hours a day: crude odds ratio 3.71 (95% CI 1.18-11.74); adjusted 32.0 (1.55-660.8).
- More than 4 hours a day: crude 3.33 (0.98-11.37, not significant); adjusted 55.8 (1.76-1737.8).

## Limitations

- Tiny sample. Confidence intervals running from under 2 to over 600 are a sign of too little data for the number of factors adjusted for. These figures should not be quoted as risk estimates.
- Retrospective recall; hospital controls; strong concern about reverse causation.

## What it does NOT show

- How much screen time changes the chance of autism, if at all.
