## In plain language

Two researchers at Drexel University proposed a theory. Infants are drawn to the bright sounds and images of screens but cannot yet recognize them as social. In some susceptible infants, they suggested, heavy early audiovisual stimulation might strengthen non-social brain pathways at the expense of social ones. They pointed to infant-directed media and recorded autism rates rising over the same years. This model is the basis for the later Heffler studies.

## Who was studied

No participants. A theoretical paper.

## What was found

- Proposes a model. It reports no data testing it.

## Limitations

- An untested hypothesis.
- Two trends rising over the same years (media use and diagnosis rates) cannot show that one drives the other. Diagnostic criteria and awareness also changed over those years.
- Later genetic, instrumental-variable and meta-analytic studies do not support a strong version of the idea (see Takahashi N 2023, Lin 2025, Cai 2025 and Ophir 2023).

## What it does NOT show

- That screens produce autism.
- That the proposed brain mechanism exists.
