## In plain language

French researchers studied 12,950 two-year-olds in ELFE, a nationally representative birth cohort. Parents answered questions on screen use (mostly TV) and an autism screening checklist (M-CHAT) by telephone. Weekly or daily screen use was very slightly associated with a medium screening score. It was associated with a lower chance of a high-risk score. The authors describe the relationship as complex.

## Who was studied

12,950 children aged 2 in France.

## What was found

- Medium M-CHAT score: weighted odds ratio 1.07 (95% CI 1.02-1.12) for weekly use and 1.05 (1.02-1.08) for daily use.
- High-risk M-CHAT score: an inverse association (exact figures [VERIFY]).

## Limitations

- Screening scores, not diagnoses.
- Screen use and screening were measured at the same age, so direction cannot be determined.
- Parent report by telephone.
- Very small effect sizes.

## What it does NOT show

- A simple "more screens, more autism" pattern. It did not find one.
