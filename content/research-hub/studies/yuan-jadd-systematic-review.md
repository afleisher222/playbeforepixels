## In plain language

This review gathered 30 studies with 356,666 participants, searching four major databases from 2006 to mid-2024. It reports a significant association between screen exposure in preschool children and autism, with longer exposure going with higher odds. It also reviews patterns of screen use and "addiction" among autistic people.

## Who was studied

30 studies, 356,666 participants.

## What was found

- A significant association between preschool screen exposure and autism, larger with longer exposure.

## Limitations

- Pools observational studies, so it cannot establish direction.
- Whether publication bias was assessed or corrected is [VERIFY]. That matters, because Ophir 2023 found the association did not survive that correction.
- Heavy overlap with the studies in Ophir 2023.
- Its "risk" wording goes further than observational data can.

## What it does NOT show

- That screens contribute to autism.
