## In plain language

Manfred Spitzer, a psychiatrist at the University of Ulm, discusses the rise in recorded autism prevalence over 50 years (from about 1 in 5,000 to 1 in 44, per the summaries). He includes broadened diagnostic concepts and greater awareness among the reasons. He also reviews Harlé's compilation of international case descriptions. The title asks whether the picture is "real or virtual autism". We could not confirm the review's overall conclusion. An earlier draft of our notes overstated it, and we corrected that.

## Who was studied

No new data.

## What was found

- Discusses rising recorded autism prevalence and includes broader diagnostic concepts and awareness among the contributors.
- Reviews Harlé's compilation of case descriptions (children under 4 with at least 4 hours a day of screens from their first year).

## Limitations

- A narrative review without systematic search methods.
- The author is a well-known public critic of screens. Our stance label rests on that known position plus the summaries, and must be checked against the text.
- Not opened. A PDF exists on the publisher's site.

## What it does NOT show

- That screens produce autism.
