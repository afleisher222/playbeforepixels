## In plain language

This review pooled 42 studies with over 18,000 children. More screen time, and especially more background TV, went with weaker language skills. Educational content and watching together with a caregiver went with stronger language skills. It points to how screens are used (together, with talk) as well as how long.

## Who was studied

42 studies, over 18,000 children. Age range [VERIFY].

## What was found

- Weaker language with more screen time and background TV; stronger language with educational content and co-viewing (effect sizes [VERIFY]).

## Limitations

- Mostly cross-sectional inputs with parent-reported exposure.
- Associations only. Families with more resources may choose different content or co-view more.

## What it does NOT show

- Anything about autism.
- That screens change language skills.
