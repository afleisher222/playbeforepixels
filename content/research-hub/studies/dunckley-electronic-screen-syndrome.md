## In plain language

A Los Angeles psychiatrist coined "Electronic Screen Syndrome", a proposed "disorder of dysregulation" of mood, attention and arousal that she links to interactive screen use. Her book claims that a strict 3-to-4-week "electronic fast" brings marked change. The label is broader than autism-like presentations but is often cited alongside "virtual autism".

## Who was studied

Clinical practice with children and adolescents. No study sample.

## What was found

- Proposes a label and a home programme. Reports no controlled data.

## Limitations

- Not peer-reviewed research. There are no controlled trials of the proposed syndrome or of the "fast".
- Not a recognized diagnosis.
- The author sells a book describing the programme.

## What it does NOT show

- That the syndrome exists as a distinct condition, or that a screen break changes children's development.
