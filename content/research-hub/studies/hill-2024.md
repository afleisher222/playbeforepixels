## In plain language

The same UC Davis group followed 82 toddlers at high or low family likelihood of autism and ADHD. Children who later had autism or ADHD concerns had significantly more screen exposure at 18 months. Across the whole group, more screen time at 18 months went with more preschool autism and ADHD traits and lower developmental scores.

## Who was studied

82 children at high or low familial likelihood for autism and ADHD, USA.

## What was found

- More screen time at 18 months among children with later autism or ADHD concerns.
- More screen time went with more preschool autism and ADHD traits and lower developmental scores.

## Limitations

- Very small sample.
- A family-history sample, which limits how far results apply to other families.
- Parent-reported screen time.
- Higher screen use at 18 months could be an early sign of emerging autism or ADHD, or a family's understandable response to a child who connects with people in different ways. The design cannot tell these apart from an effect of screens.

## What it does NOT show

- That screen time at 18 months contributes to later autism or ADHD.
