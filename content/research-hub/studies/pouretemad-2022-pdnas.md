## In plain language

Iranian researchers compared three groups of 15 young children: children with some autistic traits but no autism diagnosis who used digital devices for more than half their waking time (which the authors call "PDNAS"); autistic children; and non-autistic children. The aim was to see whether the first group could be told apart from autistic children. Secondary sources describe the authors' framing of PDNAS differently, sometimes as a proposed new subtype of autism and sometimes as a subclinical screen-associated condition.

## Who was studied

45 young children in Iran, 15 per group.

## Limitations

- Very small groups.
- Cross-sectional.
- Group membership was defined partly by screen exposure, which makes the comparison circular.
- Coins another label that uses the word "autism". PDNAS is not a recognized diagnosis.

## What it does NOT show

- That screens produced the children's traits, or that PDNAS is a distinct condition.
