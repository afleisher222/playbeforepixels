## In plain language

When Heffler et al. 2020 was published, the UK Science Media Centre gathered comments from independent researchers. One, Dr Peter Etchells, said the study "appears to add little more than noise to an already messy and controversial area of research" [VERIFY quote]. The critics pointed out that screen exposure at 12 months was a single yes/no question about TV or DVDs; that "autism-like symptoms" on a checklist are not autism, and no child was diagnosed; and that the study did not look at what was watched, the setting, or how parents and children interacted around viewing.

## Who was studied

Not a study. Comments from UK researchers on Heffler et al. 2020.

## What was found

- Screen exposure at 12 months was measured as a yes/no item [VERIFY against the study's methods].
- The outcome was a screening-checklist score, not a diagnosis.
- Content, context and parent-child interaction around viewing were not measured.
